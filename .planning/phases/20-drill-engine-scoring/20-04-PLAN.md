<objective>
Redesign the labeling drill around the platform's core purpose: making the invisible
underlying communication visible. The drill should create "holy shit moments" — where
users realize what was actually happening beneath a character's words that they missed,
and what a different response could have unlocked.

This is NOT a syntax checker. Syntax is a vehicle. The skill being trained is PERCEPTION:
learning to see the fear, the identity threat, the underlying need — before responding.

The progression:
- Level 1 (Flashcards): "Which of these best captures what's actually happening?" — trains perception
- Level 2+ (Conversational): User responds. AI reveals what was actually beneath the words.
  Character's trust/openness shifts based on whether their real need was addressed.
  After each exchange: a subtext reveal card — "What was actually there: [fear/need/identity threat]"
- Session debrief: pattern map — "You consistently responded to surface pain but missed identity threats"

Tasks 4-5 (routing + SkillsPage) are ALREADY COMMITTED (24edd26, f26dd25).

Key design decisions:
- No difficulty picker. Auto-escalation based on depth of perception, not syntax correctness.
- Each scenario has a structured subtext layer: surfacePain / underlyingFear / identityThreat.
- The subtext layer is HIDDEN during the exchange. Revealed AFTER — the learning moment.
- Character's AI response and trust_level are driven by whether their real need was seen.
- CriteriaBar shows what layer the user reached (Surface / Underlying / Identity), not just syntax ✓/✗.
- The "holy shit moment" is the reveal: "Carol's real fear was being forgotten. You responded to her anger."
</objective>
