## Chris Voss / Black Swan Techniques (Beyond Already Implemented)

| Technique | What the user does | Mode | Tier |
|-----------|-------------------|------|------|
| Late-night FM DJ voice | Uses a slow, calm, downward-inflected voice to reduce threat and soothe the counterpart | Isolated (voice drill) then both | Foundational |
| Upward inflection ("dynamic silence") | Ends phrases with a gentle upward tone to invite the other person to keep talking | Isolated then both | Foundational |
| "That's right" vs "You're right" | Guides the counterpart to articulate their view until they spontaneously say "That's right," avoiding "You're right" as a fake agreement | Integrated | Intermediate |
| "No"-oriented questions | Frames questions so the counterpart can comfortably answer "No" (e.g., "Is now a bad time?") to give them a sense of control | Isolated then both | Foundational |
| Illusion of control via calibrated questions | Uses "how" and "what" questions to make the counterpart solve your problems while feeling in charge | Both | Intermediate |
| Bending reality / loss-aversion framing | Frames outcomes in terms of what the other side stands to lose by not agreeing, rather than only what they gain | Integrated | Advanced |
| Anchoring with extreme offers | Sets an initially high or low reference point to influence the perceived range of acceptable deals | Both | Intermediate |
| The Ackerman bargaining model | Plans a target price, starts at 65%, and uses calculated, decreasing increments plus empathy tools to land near the target | Integrated (multi-turn) | Advanced |
| Deadline / time pressure use | References real or perceived deadlines to spur movement without making brittle ultimatums | Integrated | Intermediate |
| Black Swan discovery | Probes gently for unknown, high-impact information that can transform the negotiation (hidden constraints, motives, relationships) | Integrated | Advanced |
| Cold reads | Offers short, hypothesis-style observations about the counterpart's emotional state or situation to trigger correction and disclosure | Both | Intermediate |
| Paraphrasing & summarizing ("the paraphrase label") | Restates the counterpart's points and emotions in your own words until they confirm understanding | Both | Foundational |
| "Fair" framing | Uses the word "fair" strategically (and carefully) to either reassure ("I want you to feel this is fair") or flag manipulation | Integrated | Intermediate |
| Discouraging "I understand" | Avoids saying "I understand" and instead demonstrates understanding through labels and summaries | Isolated habit drill | Foundational |
| Tactical pause / silence | Uses deliberate pauses after key statements or questions to let pressure and disclosure build | Isolated then both | Foundational |
| Deference as a power move | Uses humble, deferential language and posture to lower defenses while increasing influence | Integrated | Intermediate |
| "How am I supposed to do that?" pattern | Responds to aggressive demands with calm "how" questions that force the other side to reconsider or problem-solve | Isolated then both | Foundational-Intermediate |

*(Already implemented: labeling, accusation audit, mirroring, calibrated questions, tactical empathy, strategic silence)*

## Foundational Communication Skills (Core Layer)

| Technique | Source | What the user does | Mode | Tier |
|-----------|--------|-------------------|------|------|
| Active listening | General / MI / Crucial Conversations | Gives full attention, uses brief encouragers, reflects content, and avoids interrupting | Both | Foundational |
| Reflective listening | MI | Reflects back meaning and emotion in the other person's words, sometimes adding slight inference | Both | Foundational |
| Open-ended questions | MI / Coaching | Asks "what" and "how" questions that cannot be answered with yes/no | Both | Foundational |
| Closed questions (tactical use) | General | Uses specific yes/no or short-answer questions to confirm details and commitments | Isolated then both | Foundational |
| "I" statements | Assertiveness training | States feelings, needs, and impacts from a first-person perspective instead of blaming ("I feel... when...") | Isolated then both | Foundational |
| Nonverbal awareness | General | Tracks body language, facial expression, posture, and gestures to adjust approach | Integrated | Foundational |
| Vocal tonality control | General | Deliberately adjusts volume, pitch, pace, and timbre to signal calm, curiosity, or firmness | Isolated then integrated | Foundational |
| Turn-taking and interruption control | General | Manages speaking turns, waits for cues, and gently redirects when interrupted | Both | Foundational |
| De-escalation basics | Crisis / MI | Lowers intensity using calm voice, validation, and non-threatening body language instead of matching escalation | Integrated | Foundational |
| Emotional self-regulation | General | Notices physiological arousal and uses breathing, cognitive reframing, or pausing before responding | Isolated (self-reg) then integrated | Foundational |
| Assertiveness without aggression | General | States needs and boundaries clearly while preserving respect and curiosity for the other person | Both | Foundational |
| Basic validation & affirmation | MI / General | Acknowledges efforts, strengths, and feelings in a non-patronizing way | Both | Foundational |
| Summarizing | MI / Voss | Periodically restates key points and agreements to confirm shared understanding and direction | Both | Foundational |
| Clarifying & checking understanding | General | Asks the other person if you got it right and invites corrections ("Did I miss anything important?") | Both | Foundational |
| Perspective-taking | General | Intentionally imagines the situation from the other person's view and tests that hypothesis verbally | Integrated | Foundational-Intermediate |
| Politeness / face-saving language | General | Uses softeners and respect markers to protect the other's self-image while saying something difficult | Both | Foundational |

## Motivational Interviewing (MI) Techniques

| Technique | What the user does | Mode | Tier |
|-----------|-------------------|------|------|
| OARS - Open questions | Uses open questions to invite exploration of the person's situation, values, and goals | Both | Foundational |
| OARS - Affirmations | Identifies and verbalizes strengths, efforts, and successes to support self-efficacy | Both | Foundational |
| OARS - Reflections | Reflects back content or emotion, often in slightly rephrased form to deepen insight | Both | Foundational |
| OARS - Summaries | Collects multiple reflections into a coherent recap that highlights ambivalence and progress | Both | Foundational |
| Eliciting change talk | Asks questions that draw out desire, ability, reasons, and need for change in the person's own words | Integrated | Intermediate |
| Responding to sustain talk | Reflects and explores arguments for the status quo without confronting or arguing | Integrated | Intermediate |
| Rolling with resistance | Acknowledges and joins the resistance (e.g., by reflecting) rather than opposing it, allowing momentum to shift | Integrated | Intermediate |
| Exploring ambivalence (decisional balance) | Guides the person to explore pros/cons of change and of staying the same | Both | Intermediate |
| Supporting self-efficacy | Highlights past successes and capacities to bolster confidence in making change | Both | Foundational-Intermediate |
| Emphasizing autonomy | Explicitly reinforces that decisions are the person's own and you are not the expert dictator | Integrated | Foundational |
| Focusing & agenda setting | Negotiates a focus for the conversation that aligns with the other person's priorities | Integrated | Intermediate |

## Difficult Conversations / Crucial Conversations / NVC

| Technique | Source | What the user does | Mode | Tier |
|-----------|--------|-------------------|------|------|
| Three conversations model | Difficult Conversations | Distinguishes the "what happened," "feelings," and "identity" conversations and attends to all three explicitly | Integrated | Advanced |
| Map the contribution system | Difficult Conversations | Identifies how each party has contributed to the problem, rather than assigning blame | Integrated | Intermediate |
| Reframing blame to contribution | Difficult Conversations | Recasts accusations into shared contribution language ("how we both got here") | Both | Intermediate |
| Naming the feelings conversation | Difficult Conversations | Brings unspoken feelings into the open in a direct yet non-accusatory way | Integrated | Intermediate |
| Identity work (competent / good / lovable) | Difficult Conversations | Recognizes threats to identity and uses self-talk and framing to stay grounded | Isolated reflection then integrated | Advanced |
| STATE method | Crucial Conversations | Shares facts, tells your story, asks for others' paths, talks tentatively, and encourages testing | Integrated | Intermediate |
| Mutual purpose creation | Crucial Conversations | Explicitly articulates and negotiates a goal both sides can care about | Integrated | Intermediate |
| Mutual respect maintenance | Crucial Conversations | Monitors for disrespect signals and repairs them early (apology, acknowledgment) | Integrated | Intermediate |
| Contrasting | Crucial Conversations | Clarifies what you do not intend and what you do intend to reduce perceived threat | Both | Intermediate |
| "Learn to look" (safety monitoring) | Crucial Conversations | Tracks signs that safety is at risk (silence or violence) and pauses content to restore safety | Integrated | Intermediate |
| ABC agreement skills (Agree-Build-Compare) | Crucial Conversations | First agrees where possible, then builds on, then compares differing views without invalidating | Integrated | Intermediate |
| NVC - Observation | NVC | Describes concrete behaviors or events without judgment ("When I hear you say...") | Isolated then both | Foundational |
| NVC - Feeling | NVC | Names one's own feelings rather than evaluations ("I feel concerned") | Isolated then both | Foundational |
| NVC - Need | NVC | Identifies the underlying need or value that the feeling points to | Isolated then both | Intermediate |
| NVC - Request | NVC | Formulates a clear, doable, present-moment request rather than a vague demand | Both | Intermediate |
| NVC empathy guesses | NVC | Offers tentative guesses about the other's feelings and needs for confirmation | Integrated | Intermediate |
| Making it safe before content | Crucial Conversations / NVC | Temporarily pauses problem-solving to restore safety and connection when threat is detected | Integrated | Foundational-Intermediate |

## Jim Camp Negotiation Layer

| Technique | What the user does | Mode | Tier |
|-----------|-------------------|------|------|
| Start with "No" posture | Explicitly gives the counterpart permission to say "no" and treats it as a safe, stabilizing decision rather than a failure | Integrated | Intermediate |
| Eliminate neediness | Consciously detaches from outcome, focusing on executing the process so emotional "need" cannot be used against them | Isolated mindset drill then integrated | Advanced |
| Vision-building questions | Asks questions that build a vivid vision of the counterpart's better future if they accept, and consequences if they don't | Integrated | Advanced |
| No-tolerance for "maybe" | Refuses to park in "maybe" and gently pushes ambiguous answers toward a clear yes/no decision | Integrated | Intermediate |
| Decision-based negotiation | Structures conversations around helping the other party make clear decisions rather than chasing agreement | Integrated | Intermediate |

## PREP and Opinion-Giving Structures

| Technique | Source | What the user does | Mode | Tier |
|-----------|--------|-------------------|------|------|
| PREP: Point-Reason-Example-Point | Toastmasters / corporate | States a clear conclusion, gives 1-3 reasons, offers a concrete example, and restates the conclusion | Isolated then integrated | Foundational |
| PREP "one-breath point" | Same | Practices stating the core point in a single concise sentence before elaborating | Isolated | Foundational |
| PREP under pressure (impromptu) | Same | Applies PREP in real time when cold-called for an opinion or update | Integrated | Intermediate |

Variants:
- P-R-2E-P (multiple examples to different stakeholders) - intermediate
- Point-Risk-Mitigation-Point (for pushing a controversial recommendation) - advanced derivative

## "What - So What - Now What" & Reflection Frameworks

| Technique | Source | What the user does | Mode | Tier |
|-----------|--------|-------------------|------|------|
| What? So What? Now What? | Borton / Rolfe reflective model | Describes what happened, interprets why it matters, then identifies concrete next actions | Both (post-roleplay debrief) | Foundational-Intermediate |
| "What" factual description | Same | Trains themselves to describe events neutrally before interpreting | Isolated | Foundational |
| "So what" meaning-making | Same | Analyzes implications, patterns, and emotional reactions | Isolated then integrated | Intermediate |
| "Now what" action planning | Same | Converts insights into specific behavior changes or experiments | Both | Intermediate |
| Gibbs' Reflective Cycle (lite) | Gibbs / higher-ed | Moves through description, feelings, evaluation, analysis, conclusion, and action plan when debriefing a tough interaction | Integrated (post-session) | Advanced |

## Harvard / MBA Structuring Frameworks

| Framework | Source | What the user does | Mode | Tier |
|-----------|--------|-------------------|------|------|
| Minto Pyramid / SCQA | McKinsey / case teaching | Opens with the answer, then supports it with grouped reasons, often framed as Situation-Complication-Question-Answer | Isolated writing/speaking drills then integrated | Advanced |
| Situation-Complication-Resolution (SCR) | Case method / consulting | Describes the situation, introduces the tension/complication, then proposes a resolution | Integrated (presentations, escalations) | Intermediate |
| Issue-Recommendation-Support (IRS) | MBA-style memo frameworks | States the central issue, gives a clear recommendation, and then supports with data and reasoning | Isolated then integrated | Intermediate |
| Pros-Cons-Recommendation | Business schools, exec ed | Lays out key pros, key cons, then a recommendation with rationale | Both | Foundational-Intermediate |
| Three-point "rule of 3" structuring | Rhetoric / public speaking | Organizes support into three parallel points for memorability and clarity | Isolated | Foundational |
| Executive summary first (BLUF) | Military / policy / Harvard case writing | Starts with the bottom line (decision/request), then provides necessary context and justification | Integrated | Intermediate |
| Stakeholder-tailored framing | Case teaching / corporate | Repackages the same recommendation so that the Point/Reason examples speak directly to each stakeholder's priorities | Integrated | Advanced |

## Clustering Guidance

- **PREP, SCQA, Pros-Cons-Recommendation, BLUF** = single "structured opinion/briefing" cluster
- **What-So What-Now What / Gibbs** = reflective counterpart for after drills and real conversations
- **Camp** = negotiation mindset + decision architecture layer that complements Voss and MI rather than duplicating them
