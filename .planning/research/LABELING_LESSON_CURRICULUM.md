### LESSON L1: Affect-Shift Labels -- The Foundation (Labels 1-3, Beginner)

#### 1. Concept Introduction

**What:** Affect-shift Labels are observations about changes in someone's emotional state -- shifts in tone, pace, volume, pauses, or verbal energy. They use the formula "It seems/sounds/looks like..." followed by a neutral observation about what just shifted.

**Why:** When you name what changed in someone's demeanor, three things happen neurobiologically: (1) their right ventrolateral prefrontal cortex activates, giving them conscious access to their own emotion; (2) their amygdala reactivity decreases by 0.3-0.6 units; (3) they feel compelled to confirm, correct, or elaborate -- all of which give you information you did not have before. MI research validates this: complex reflections (the MI parallel to labels) predict change talk at r=0.4-0.5 (MITI strength), with %Complex >40% as the proficiency benchmark (PMC5361059, MITI 4.0). Meta-analyses show MI outperforms controls at d=0.18-0.52 via the same mechanics -- reflections + silence (PMC8200683).

**When:** Whenever you notice ANY shift -- a pause where there was flow, a change in vocal energy, a new hesitation, a sudden formality, laughter that sounds forced, or silence after a direct statement. The shift is the signal. You do not need to know what caused it. The Label finds out.

**Source:** Chris Voss, Never Split the Difference. Black Swan Group Go-To Labels 1-3: "It looks like something just crossed your mind." / "It seems like you're uncomfortable with that." / "It seems like you're hesitant."

#### 2. Core Mechanics

**Exact Verbal Formula:**
- "It seems like [observation about the shift]."
- "It sounds like [observation about the shift]."
- "It looks like [observation about the shift]."

**Critical Syntax Rules:**
- Subject is "it" -- never "I" or "you"
- Statement form -- never end with "?" or "right?"
- No hedging: remove "maybe," "kind of," "sort of," "I think"
- No explaining after the Label -- silence does the work

**Delivery Requirements:**
- **Pace:** 110-140 WPM (FM-DJ voice range). Significantly slower than normal conversational pace (160-190 WPM).
- **Pitch:** 0.5-1.5 semitones below your conversational baseline. Not a caricature baritone -- just slightly lower, so the listener leans in.
- **Inflection:** Two acceptable patterns for affect-shift Labels:
  - Default: Downward inflection on the final word. Projects certainty and calm. "It seems like you're hesitant." (voice drops on "hesitant")
  - Variant: Short hesitation after "It seems like," then curious upward inflection on the last word. Invites exploration. "It seems like... you're hesitant?" (gentle rise, genuine curiosity)
- **Silence:** Minimum 3 seconds after delivery. Count "one-one-thousand, two-one-thousand, three-one-thousand." Do NOT speak until they respond. Typical range: 3-12 seconds.

**Common Mistakes with Examples:**

| Mistake | Novice Example | Why It Fails | Expert Correction |
|---------|---------------|--------------|-------------------|
| Using "I" frame | "I'm hearing that you're frustrated." | Makes it about your perception -- they defend against YOU | "It sounds like something about this is frustrating." |
| Using "You" frame | "You seem upset about the timeline." | Direct accusation -- triggers defensiveness | "It seems like the timeline landed differently than expected." |
| Turning into question | "It seems like you're hesitant, right?" | "Right?" seeks validation; weakens the observation | "It seems like you're hesitant." [PAUSE] |
| Surface emotion only | "It seems like you're frustrated." | "Frustrated" is too broad -- could be impatient, threatened, overwhelmed | "It seems like you're concerned this puts you in a difficult position." |
| Breaking silence | "It seems like you're hesitant. I mean, I just want to understand..." | Fills the space the listener needs to process and respond | "It seems like you're hesitant." [COUNT TO 3+ AND WAIT] |
| Hedging | "I think maybe it sort of seems like you might be, um, concerned?" | Fillers and qualifiers destroy conviction; listener cannot trust the observation | "It sounds like you're concerned about the impact." |

**Expert vs Novice Contrast Pairs:**

Scenario: Manager pauses after you ask for a raise, then says "Let me think about it."

- **Novice:** "It seems like you're uncertain." (Surface. Generic. Tells them nothing they don't already know.)
- **Expert:** "It sounds like you want to make sure you're making the right call here." (Names their constraint: control and correctness. They feel understood.)

Scenario: Colleague's tone becomes formal/cold during a project discussion.

- **Novice:** "It seems like you're upset." (Accusatory even with correct syntax. "Upset" is a judgment.)
- **Expert:** "It seems like I've stepped into something you weren't expecting." (Takes responsibility for the shift. Non-accusatory. Invites correction.)

#### 3. Practice Exercises

**Exercise 1: Syntax Lock-In (Beginner)**
- **Task:** Listen to scenario audio. Record a label using correct "It seems/sounds/looks like..." syntax.
- **Scenarios:** salary-001, difficult-001, sayno-001 (existing beginner scenarios)
- **Scoring:** Syntax only (0-40). Must hit: correct opener (15 pts), no I-framing (10 pts), no You-framing (5 pts), statement not question (10 pts).
- **Feedback:** If syntax < 30, replay with specific correction. "You started with 'I'm hearing' -- try 'It sounds like' instead."

**Exercise 2: Surface vs Depth (Beginner)**
- **Task:** Listen to scenario. Record a label that names the UNDERLYING DRIVER, not the surface emotion.
- **Scenarios:** salary-002, workplace-001, difficult-002
- **Scoring:** Depth (0-60). Surface emotion only = 25 pts max. Underlying driver = 45-60 pts.
- **Feedback:** "You labeled 'frustrated' -- that's the surface. What's DRIVING the frustration? Think about what they stand to lose."

**Exercise 3: Full Label + Silence (Beginner)**
- **Task:** Record a complete label AND hold silence for minimum 3 seconds.
- **Scenarios:** salary-001, sayno-001, workplace-001
- **Scoring:** Combined syntax (0-40) + depth (0-60) + silence bonus (+5 for 3-5s, +10 for 5-8s, +15 for 8-12s).
- **Feedback:** If silence < 3s: "You spoke again after [X]s. The silence is where the magic happens -- count to three-one-thousand before saying anything."

#### 4. Mastery Criteria

- **Score threshold:** >= 65 overall on 3 consecutive attempts
- **Syntax minimum:** Correct opener in all 3 attempts
- **Silence minimum:** >= 3 seconds Dynamic Silence in at least 2 of 3 attempts
- **Attempt minimum:** At least 5 total attempts before mastery can be achieved (prevents lucky streaks)
- **What "done" looks like:** User consistently produces labels with correct syntax, attempts to name an emotion or driver (even if surface-level), and holds silence for 3+ seconds.
- **Unlocks:** L2 (Affect Shifts Intermediate), L4 (Behavior Probes Beginner), L6 (Opening Labels)

#### 5. Integration Notes

- **Chaining:** After mastering basic Labels, the next technique in the N9 sequence is Mirrors. A common chain: Mirror their last 1-3 words -> Silence -> Label the response. This is practiced in L3.
- **Common sequences:** Label -> Dynamic Silence -> counterpart responds -> Mirror their response -> Label again (the Label-Mirror-Label loop).
- **When to switch tools:** If the counterpart responds with minimal/stonewalled answers after a good Label + Silence, move to a behavior probe (Labels 4-5) rather than stacking more affect-shift Labels. Over-labeling (5+ in a row) appears manipulative.

### LESSON L2: Affect-Shift Labels -- Depth & Specificity (Labels 1-3, Intermediate)

#### 1. Concept Introduction

**What:** Moving beyond naming the obvious emotion to identifying the UNDERLYING DRIVER -- the specific fear, need, or constraint that produces the surface emotion. "Frustrated" is a surface emotion. "Worried about setting a precedent you can't control" is the driver.

**Why:** Surface labels ("It seems like you're frustrated") produce acknowledgment -- the listener nods. Underlying driver labels ("It seems like you're worried this sets a precedent") produce revelation -- the listener opens up, corrects, or shares something they were not planning to share. The difference in amygdala reduction is measurable: accurate, specific labels produce stronger RVLPFC activation than generic ones. MI confirms this depth gradient: simple reflections (surface paraphrase) build rapport (d=0.41 for contemplation increase), while complex reflections (adding meaning/emotion) predict change talk at r=0.4-0.5 -- the shift from L1 surface to L2 driver maps directly to MI's simple-to-complex progression (PMC5018199, MITI 4.0).

**When:** Every time. Once syntax is locked in (L1), every label attempt should aim for the driver, not the surface.

**Source:** Black Swan Group Structured Taxonomy -- Surface vs Underlying Emotion mapping. Voss: "Linguistic precision is the difference between a novice and an expert."

#### 2. Core Mechanics

**The Depth Stack (surface -> driver -> identity):**

| Layer | Example | Impact |
|-------|---------|--------|
| Surface emotion | "It seems like you're frustrated." | Acknowledged -- they feel seen |
| Underlying driver | "It seems like you're concerned about how this looks to leadership." | Understood -- they feel perspective-taking |
| Identity/core need | "It seems like being seen as someone who controls outcomes matters deeply here." | Deeply connected -- they reveal vulnerabilities |

**Driver Identification Framework:**
For any surface emotion, ask internally: "What do they stand to LOSE?" Common driver categories:
- Control/autonomy: "...concerned about losing control of..."
- Status/reputation: "...worried about how this looks to..."
- Fairness/precedent: "...concerned this sets a precedent..."
- Security/predictability: "...uncertain about what happens next..."
- Competence/credibility: "...want to make sure you've thought through..."
- Belonging/relationship: "...worried this changes how we work together..."

**Tense Selection (refining from L1):**
- Present continuous ("...you're worried...") -- active emotional state happening now
- Present simple ("...you're concerned about...") -- broader dispositional state
- Future possibility ("...you might be hesitant about...") -- pre-emptive, less accusatory

**Delivery Refinement:**
- FM-DJ voice: same 110-140 WPM, pitch slightly below baseline
- Opener variation: alternate between "It seems like," "It sounds like," and "It looks like" across attempts. Using the same opener every time signals template rigidity (Pattern Type B from research).
- Dynamic Silence target: 4+ seconds (increased from L1's 3 seconds)

**Common Mistakes (L2-specific):**

| Mistake | Example | Correction |
|---------|---------|------------|
| Content labeling | "It seems like the price is the problem." | "It seems like the value proposition hasn't landed yet." (Emotion, not content) |
| Judgmental framing | "It sounds like you're being unreasonable." | "It seems like the timeline feels tighter than you expected." (Neutral observation) |
| Over-specificity (guessing wrong) | "It seems like you're worried about getting fired." | "It seems like there's something at stake here that hasn't been addressed." (Broader when uncertain) |

**Expert vs Novice Contrast Pairs:**

Scenario: Your manager's tone becomes colder after you object to a project timeline.

- **Novice:** "It seems like you're upset." (Surface. Judgmental. "Upset" is YOUR read, not their experience.)
- **Expert:** "It seems like I've stepped into territory you weren't expecting." (Takes ownership of the shift. Names the surprise, not the emotion. Invites them to clarify what the territory IS.)

Scenario: A colleague says "Whatever you think is best" with a shrug.

- **Novice:** "It seems like you don't care." (Accusatory. Shuts them down.)
- **Expert:** "It seems like your input hasn't felt like it's made a difference." (Names the REASON for the disengagement. They feel understood.)

#### 3. Practice Exercises

**Exercise 1: Surface-to-Driver Rewrite (Rewrite Text format)**
- **Task:** Given a novice label, rewrite it to target the underlying driver.
- **Scenarios:**
  - Novice: "It seems like you're frustrated." -> Rewrite targeting control/autonomy
  - Novice: "It sounds like you're worried." -> Rewrite targeting specific fear
  - Novice: "It looks like you're hesitant." -> Rewrite targeting what they stand to lose
- **Scoring:** Original uses surface emotion = 0 pts. Rewrite contains underlying driver keyword = 45-60 pts depth.
- **Feedback:** Show the expert example after each attempt. "Your rewrite: [X]. Expert version: [Y]. Notice how the expert names the specific CONSTRAINT, not just the emotion."

**Exercise 2: Depth Under Pressure (Prompt-Response Audio)**
- **Task:** Listen to intermediate-difficulty scenario audio. Record a label targeting the underlying driver. Time pressure: 10 seconds to formulate after the scenario statement ends.
- **Scenarios:** salary-003, salary-004, difficult-003, workplace-002
- **Scoring:** Syntax (0-40) + Depth (0-60). Depth must be >= 40 to pass.
- **Feedback:** If depth < 40: "You named the emotion but not the driver. Ask yourself: what does this person stand to LOSE? That's the driver."

**Exercise 3: Opener Variation Drill (Audio-Spoken Delivery)**
- **Task:** Practice the SAME scenario three times, using a different opener each time ("It seems like...", "It sounds like...", "It looks like...").
- **Scenarios:** salary-002, difficult-002, sayno-003
- **Scoring:** Syntax (must use correct and DIFFERENT opener each time) + Depth (aim for driver) + Silence (4+ seconds).
- **Feedback:** If same opener used twice: "You used 'It seems like' both times. Varying your opener prevents template rigidity and sounds more natural."

#### 4. Mastery Criteria

- **Score threshold:** >= 70 overall on 3 consecutive attempts
- **Depth minimum:** Underlying driver identified in at least 2 of 3 attempts (depth >= 45)
- **Opener variation:** At least 2 different openers used across the 3 attempts
- **Silence minimum:** >= 4 seconds Dynamic Silence in at least 2 of 3
- **Attempt minimum:** At least 8 total attempts
- **What "done" looks like:** User consistently identifies underlying drivers (not just surface emotions), varies their opener, and holds 4+ seconds of silence.
- **Unlocks:** L3 (Affect Shifts Advanced)

#### 5. Integration Notes

- **Vocabulary tracking starts here:** From L2 onward, the system tracks TTR and MTLD across attempts. If TTR < 0.4 after 5+ attempts, surface a nudge: "You're reusing the same emotional vocabulary. Try these driver words: [3 unused words from the UNDERLYING_DRIVERS list]."
- **Pattern detection:** Flag Pattern Type B (same template structure with only adjective swapped). Feedback: "You're using the same sentence structure each time. Try restructuring: instead of 'It seems like you're [adjective],' try 'It seems like [situation] is creating [driver].'"
- **Chains with Mirrors:** After L2, users can practice the Mirror -> Label sequence. Mirror their last 1-3 words, hold silence, then Label whatever they reveal.

### LESSON L3: Affect-Shift Labels -- Nuance & Chaining (Labels 1-3, Advanced)

#### 1. Concept Introduction

**What:** Advanced affect-shift labeling combines three skills: (1) identity-level depth in the Label itself, (2) Label-Mirror-Label chaining for progressive discovery, and (3) correct inflection selection (downward for authority vs. upward-curious for exploration).

**Why:** At this level, the goal is not just to name an emotion or driver but to reach the IDENTITY layer -- the core need or self-concept at stake. Identity-level labels ("It seems like being seen as someone who delivers matters deeply here") create psychological safety so profound that the counterpart reveals information they were not planning to share. This is the "That's right!" moment -- the gold standard. MI's double-sided reflections -- capturing both sides of ambivalence ("Health risks but stress relief -- and?") -- are the closest MI parallel to identity labels, and produce the highest scores for discrepancy exploration (MI global score increase, p<0.001). Language style synchrony at this depth predicts empathy ratings at OR=2.4 (PMC5018199).

**When:** After establishing rapport through L1-L2 labeling. Identity labels are appropriate when you have enough context to make a deep observation, NOT as opening moves.

**Source:** Black Swan Group mastery markers -- "That's right!" (not "You're right") as the confirmation signal. N9 sequence: Label -> Mirror -> Label loops.

#### 2. Core Mechanics

**Identity-Level Labeling:**
The identity layer names WHO they are trying to be, not just what they feel:

| Driver Label (L2) | Identity Label (L3) |
|-------------------|---------------------|
| "...concerned about how this looks to leadership" | "...being seen as someone who protects the team matters here" |
| "...worried about setting a precedent" | "...your role as the person who maintains standards is at stake" |
| "...uncertain about the impact" | "...making decisions you can stand behind is important to you" |

**Label-Mirror-Label Chaining:**
1. Label: "It seems like there's something about this timeline that's creating pressure." [SILENCE 3-5s]
2. Counterpart responds: "Well, it's just that my boss is already asking questions about deliverables."
3. Mirror: "...asking questions about deliverables?" [SILENCE 3-5s]
4. Counterpart elaborates: "Yeah, she's worried we're falling behind on Q2 targets."
5. Label (deeper): "It sounds like you're carrying the weight of the whole team's performance here." [SILENCE 5+s]
6. Target response: "That's right. That's exactly what's happening."

**Inflection Selection:**
- **Downward inflection** (default): Use when you have high confidence in your observation. Projects authority and calm certainty. "It seems like you're carrying the weight of the whole team's performance." (Voice drops on "performance.")
- **Curious upward inflection** (variant): Use when you are probing an unclear shift. Invites exploration. "It seems like... something just shifted for you?" (Gentle rise on "you," genuine curiosity.)
- **Rule:** Downward for Labels you are confident about. Upward for Labels where you are genuinely uncertain what caused the shift.

**"That's Right!" vs "You're Right" Distinction:**
- "That's right!" = They feel deeply understood. Your Label landed at the identity level. This is the WIN.
- "You're right." = They are dismissing you. They want you to stop talking. This is NOT the win.
- The app should help users distinguish these responses in practice scenarios.

**Common Mistakes (L3-specific):**

| Mistake | Example | Correction |
|---------|---------|------------|
| Over-labeling (5+ in a row) | Stacking Labels without Mirroring or Questions | After 2-3 Labels, switch to a Mirror or Calibrated Question |
| Wrong inflection for context | Curious upward inflection on an impasse | Downward inflection for certainty; upward only for genuine exploration |
| Identity Label too early | Opening with "It seems like being respected matters to you" | Build through surface -> driver -> identity over multiple exchanges |

#### 3. Practice Exercises

**Exercise 1: Identity Upgrade (Audio-Spoken Delivery)**
- **Task:** Given a driver-level label (provided as text), upgrade it to identity-level and record with correct delivery.
- **Examples:**
  - Given: "It seems like you're concerned about the team dynamic." -> Upgrade to identity level
  - Given: "It sounds like you're worried about losing credibility." -> Upgrade to identity level
  - Given: "It looks like the timing feels rushed." -> Upgrade to identity level
- **Scoring:** Depth (must be 'highly-specific' = 60 pts), delivery (FM-DJ compliance, correct inflection), silence (5+ seconds).

**Exercise 2: Chain Building (Prompt-Response Audio)**
- **Task:** Multi-turn exercise. User Labels -> AI responds -> User Mirrors -> AI elaborates -> User Labels deeper.
- **Scenarios:** salary-004, difficult-004, workplace-002
- **Scoring:** Label quality on each turn, progressive depth (each subsequent Label should score higher on depth), silence maintained between turns.
- **Feedback:** "Your first Label scored 55 (surface). Your second scored 72 (driver). Aim for 80+ (identity) on your third."

**Exercise 3: Inflection Recognition (Spot-the-Technique)**
- **Task:** Listen to audio clips of Labels delivered with different inflections. Identify: (a) which inflection was used, (b) whether it was appropriate for the context.
- **Clips:** 5 clips alternating downward and upward-curious, some matched to context, some mismatched.
- **Scoring:** Correct identification of inflection type (50%) + correct judgment of context-appropriateness (50%).

#### 4. Mastery Criteria

- **Score threshold:** >= 75 overall on 3 consecutive attempts
- **Depth minimum:** At least 1 of 3 attempts reaches 'highly-specific' (depth = 60)
- **TTR minimum:** >= 0.5 across all session attempts (vocabulary diversity)
- **Silence minimum:** >= 5 seconds Dynamic Silence in at least 2 of 3
- **Inflection:** Correct inflection selection in Spot-the-Technique exercise (>= 80%)
- **What "done" looks like:** User produces varied, deep labels that reach the driver or identity layer, uses appropriate inflection for context, chains Labels with Mirrors, and holds extended silence.
- **Unlocks:** L8 (Impasse Labels) -- in combination with L5 and L7

#### 5. Integration Notes

- **Post-L3, user is ready for full N9 sequence drills:** Mirror -> Label -> Calibrated Question. These will be practiced in the Simulation Studio, not Skills Lab drills.
- **"That's right!" tracking:** From L3 onward, AI counterpart responses include "That's right!" for labels scoring >= 80. This trains the user to recognize the gold standard response.
- **Mis-Label awareness seeding:** In L3 exercises, occasionally mention that getting a correction from the counterpart is ALSO valuable -- it surfaces information. This seeds the Mis-Label concept without teaching it explicitly.

### LESSON L4: Behavior Probe Labels -- "Reason for..." (Labels 4-5, Beginner)

#### 1. Concept Introduction

**What:** Behavior Probe Labels are observations about someone's ACTIONS or STATEMENTS, not their emotional state. They use the specific formula: "It seems like you have a reason for [saying/doing that]." This replaces accusatory questions like "Why did you do that?" or "What did you mean by that?"

**Why:** "Why" questions are accusatory -- they trigger defensiveness. "It seems like you have a reason for that" assumes positive intent and invites explanation. The difference: "Why" demands justification. "Reason for" assumes there IS a reason and creates space to share it. In the Black Swan example, a general contractor used "It seems like you have a reason for not doing the work?" and the subcontractor responded "...No, I don't" -- and the deal was worked out. MI's simple reflections (the parallel to behavior probes) build rapport with d=0.41 for contemplation increase and boost processing/responding skills (pre-post delta=1.03, p<0.01) -- confirming that non-accusatory probing opens doors that direct questions close (PMC12677012).

**When:** When someone has SAID something unexpected or DONE something you dislike or don't understand. The trigger is VERBAL BEHAVIOR or ACTION, not a non-verbal affect shift (that's Labels 1-3). Distinction: Labels 1-3 = non-verbal/tonal shifts. Labels 4-5 = verbal statements and observable actions.

**Source:** Black Swan Group Go-To Labels 4-5. Label 4: "It seems like you have a reason for saying that." Label 5: "It seems like you have a reason for [naming the specific action]."

#### 2. Core Mechanics

**Exact Verbal Formula:**
- Label 4: "It seems like you have a reason for saying that."
- Label 5: "It seems like you have a reason for [specific action named]."

**Critical Distinction from Labels 1-3:**

| Feature | Labels 1-3 (Affect Shift) | Labels 4-5 (Behavior Probe) |
|---------|--------------------------|----------------------------|
| Trigger | Non-verbal/tonal shift | Verbal statement or action |
| Focus | Emotional state | Behavioral reason |
| Formula | "It seems like you're [emotion]." | "It seems like you have a reason for [behavior]." |
| Risk if wrong category | Assumes emotion wrongly -> defensive, HARMFUL | Ignores non-verbal signals -> less effective |

**Naming the Behavior (Label 5 specifics):**
The power of Label 5 is in naming the SPECIFIC action, not the generic behavior:
- Weak: "It seems like you have a reason for doing that."
- Strong: "It seems like you have a reason for moving the deadline."
- Strongest: "It seems like you have a reason for moving the deadline without checking with the team."

**Delivery Requirements:**
- FM-DJ voice: 110-140 WPM, pitch slightly below baseline
- Inflection: Either downward (firm empathic read) or curious upward (exploratory). Same as Labels 1-3.
- Silence: Minimum 3 seconds. Critical for behavior probes because the counterpart needs processing time to formulate their explanation.
- Tone: MUST be genuinely curious, not accusatory. "Your inner voice drives your outer voice" -- if you are judging them internally, it leaks into your tone.

**Common Mistakes:**

| Mistake | Example | Correction |
|---------|---------|------------|
| Using "Why" instead | "Why did you move the deadline?" | "It seems like you have a reason for moving the deadline." |
| Labeling emotion when behavior probe is needed | "It seems like you're frustrated" (when they skipped work) | "It seems like you have a reason for not completing the deliverable." |
| Not naming the specific behavior | "It seems like you have a reason for that." | "It seems like you have a reason for canceling the meeting." |
| Accusatory tone | *said with irritation* "It seems like you have a reason..." | *genuinely curious* "It seems like you have a reason..." (inner voice: "I'm genuinely curious") |

#### 3. Practice Exercises

**Exercise 1: "Why" to "Reason For" Conversion (Multiple-Choice Recognition)**
- **Task:** Given a scenario and a "Why" question, select the correct behavior probe label from 4 options.
- **Scenarios:**
  - "Why did you skip the meeting?" -> Identify best Label 4/5
  - "Why did you say that to the client?" -> Identify best Label 4/5
  - "Why didn't you finish the report?" -> Identify best Label 4/5
- **Scoring:** Correct selection = pass. Must select 3 of 4 correctly.

### LESSON L5: Behavior Probe Labels -- Actions & Statements (Labels 4-5, Intermediate)

#### 1. Concept Introduction

**What:** Intermediate behavior probing moves beyond the "reason for saying/doing" template to naming the specific IMPACT of the behavior and creating space for the counterpart to explain their internal logic. This is where Labels 4-5 become sophisticated tools for non-accusatory confrontation.

**Why:** At the beginner level, "It seems like you have a reason for [action]" opens the door. At the intermediate level, you name the specific behavioral pattern, its observable impact, and create genuine curiosity about the logic behind it. This produces richer explanations and deeper trust. MI research shows that as reflection specificity increases, the CT:ST (change talk to sustain talk) ratio improves -- behavior probes that name the exact action move beyond simple rapport-building toward evoking Reason-category change talk, which has the strongest meta-analytic signal for behavior change (r=0.16, PMC6310665).

**When:** When someone has a pattern of behavior (not a one-off), when the behavior has clear downstream effects, or when the statement they made carries implicit meaning worth surfacing.

**Source:** Black Swan Group -- "Gets them out of that behavior without making them defensive." The subcontractor example demonstrates the power: naming the behavior + silence = resolution without argument.

#### 2. Core Mechanics

**Advanced Behavior Naming:**

| Basic (L4) | Advanced (L5) |
|------------|---------------|
| "...reason for not finishing the report." | "...reason for prioritizing [X] over the report." |
| "...reason for saying that." | "...reason for framing it that way to the client." |
| "...reason for skipping the meeting." | "...reason for stepping back from the project review." |

The advanced form names the SPECIFIC behavioral choice, not just the absence or presence of action.

**Statement Probing (Label 4 advanced):**
When someone says something unexpected, probe the statement itself:
- "It seems like you chose those words deliberately."
- "It seems like there's a reason you framed it as [their exact phrase]."
- "It seems like mentioning [specific thing they said] was important."

**Delivery Refinement:**
- Tone must be genuinely curious -- not performative curiosity. "Your inner voice drives your outer voice." Practice internal script: "I'm genuinely curious about their reasoning" before speaking.
- Silence extends to 4+ seconds for behavior probes because the counterpart needs time to formulate an explanation (more cognitive load than emotional response).
- Pre-Label micro-pause: 300-500ms silence before Label onset to reset your tone.

**Common Mistakes (L5-specific):**

| Mistake | Example | Correction |
|---------|---------|------------|
| Naming impact as accusation | "It seems like you don't care about the team." | "It seems like there's something about the team dynamic that's influencing your approach." |
| Stacking behavior probes | Three Label 5s in a row | After 2 behavior probes, switch to Mirror or Label 1-3 |
| Generic behavior naming | "...reason for doing that." | "...reason for choosing to handle it offline instead of in the meeting." |

#### 3. Practice Exercises

**Exercise 1: Specificity Upgrade (Rewrite Text)**
- **Task:** Given a basic Label 4/5, rewrite to name the specific behavioral choice.
- **Examples:**
  - "It seems like you have a reason for not coming." -> Rewrite to name specific avoidance
  - "It seems like you have a reason for saying that." -> Rewrite to reference their specific words
  - "It seems like you have a reason for the delay." -> Rewrite to name the prioritization
- **Scoring:** Specificity of behavior named. Generic = 25 pts. Specific action = 45 pts. Specific choice/pattern = 60 pts.

**Exercise 2: Statement Probing (Prompt-Response Audio)**
- **Task:** Listen to a scenario where someone makes an unexpected statement. Record a Label 4 that probes the statement.
- **Scenarios:** behavior-004 through behavior-006 (new scenarios from Part 3)
- **Scoring:** Syntax + Depth (must reference the specific statement) + Silence (4+ seconds)

**Exercise 3: Inner Voice Calibration (Audio-Spoken Delivery)**
- **Task:** Record the same Label 5 twice: first with a hostile inner narrative ("They're being lazy"), then with a curious inner narrative ("I genuinely want to understand their thinking"). Compare playback.
- **Purpose:** Demonstrate how "your inner voice drives your outer voice" affects delivery even with identical words.
- **Scoring:** Tone difference between recordings (pace, pitch variation, harshness markers).

#### 4. Mastery Criteria

- **Score threshold:** >= 70 on 3 consecutive attempts
- **Behavior naming:** Specific behavior/choice named (not generic) in 2 of 3
- **Silence minimum:** >= 4 seconds in 2 of 3
- **Tone:** No accusatory tone markers detected (sharp pitch spikes, increased WPM > 140)

- **Unlocks:** L8 (Impasse Labels) -- in combination with L3 and L7

### LESSON L6: Opening Labels -- "Place You Want to Start" (Label 7)

#### 1. Concept Introduction

**What:** The Opening Label replaces the typical "How are you?" / "Fine, how are you?" social script. Instead of this ritual, you observe that the other person has an agenda and create space for them to begin: "It sounds like you have a place you want to start."

**Why:** Most people who ask "How are you?" are not asking about your wellbeing -- they are testing whether you are ready to listen. They have already thought through what they want to discuss. By labeling their readiness, you: (1) skip the performative pleasantry, (2) demonstrate that you are paying attention, and (3) hand them the floor immediately. Most people are relieved. Those who want chitchat circle back naturally -- nothing is lost. MI's facilitative silences and open-ended reflections serve the same function -- creating space for the client to lead. Context-aware silences increase disclosure (p<0.05 vs. static pacing), and the MITI proficiency benchmark of reflection:question ratio >1:1 reinforces that giving the floor (via reflection, not questions) produces richer engagement (arxiv 2602.06134v1).

**When:** At the opening of any planned conversation: meetings, negotiations, difficult conversations, 1-on-1s. NOT for casual social encounters where rapport-building IS the point.

**Source:** Black Swan Group Go-To Label 7: "It sounds like you have a place you want to start."

#### 2. Core Mechanics

**Exact Verbal Formula:**
- "It sounds like you have a place you want to start."
- Variations: "It seems like you've got something on your mind." / "It looks like there's something you want to get to."

**Delivery Requirements:**
- **Tone:** Encouraging and warm. This is NOT the FM-DJ "Analyst" tone. Label 7 is the exception -- warmth takes priority over calm authority.
- **Inflection:** Neither strictly downward nor upward. The priority is warmth and openness.
- **Pace:** Slightly slower than conversational but not as deliberately slow as FM-DJ. Aim for 130-150 WPM.
- **Silence:** 3+ seconds after delivery. They need space to decide whether to jump in or circle back to pleasantries.
- **"Your inner voice drives your outer voice":** Internal script before Label 7: "I'm genuinely interested in what's on their mind and ready to listen."

**Critical Distinction from Other Labels:**

| Feature | Labels 1-5 | Label 7 (Opening) |
|---------|-----------|-------------------|
| Tone | FM-DJ calm/observant | Warm/encouraging |
| Inflection | Downward or curious-upward | Warm and open |
| Context | Mid-conversation, responding to signals | Conversation opener |
| Follow-up | Dynamic Silence + potential Mirror/Label | Give them the floor |

**Common Mistakes:**

| Mistake | Example | Correction |
|---------|---------|------------|
| Using FM-DJ tone on Label 7 | *cold, clinical* "It sounds like you have a place you want to start." | *warm, encouraging* "It sounds like you have a place you want to start." |
| Using it in casual social settings | Opening a party conversation with Label 7 | Reserve for structured/purposeful conversations |
| Breaking silence to redirect | "It sounds like you have a place you want to start. So what's on your mind?" | Label 7 + SILENCE. Let them choose to speak. |
| Sounding performative | Using it as a "technique" without genuine curiosity | Only use when you genuinely believe they have something to say |

#### 3. Practice Exercises

**Exercise 1: Opening Recognition (Multiple-Choice Recognition)**
- **Task:** Listen to 4 conversation openings. Identify which ones would benefit from a Label 7 vs standard social response.
- **Scenarios:** Mix of structured meetings, casual encounters, 1-on-1s, and negotiations
- **Scoring:** Correct context identification (when to use Label 7 vs not)

**Exercise 2: Warm Delivery (Audio-Spoken Delivery)**
- **Task:** Record Label 7 with warm, encouraging tone. System evaluates tone (should NOT match FM-DJ profile).
- **Scenarios:** opening-001 through opening-004 (new scenarios from Part 3)
- **Scoring:** Warm tone detected (NOT low-arousal FM-DJ), pace in 130-150 WPM range, silence >= 3 seconds
- **Feedback:** If FM-DJ detected: "Your tone is too clinical for an opening Label. Think warmth, not authority. Try again with the inner script: 'I'm genuinely interested in what they have to say.'"

**Exercise 3: Full Opening Sequence (Prompt-Response Audio)**
- **Task:** Listen to a scenario where someone starts a meeting/conversation. Record your Label 7 response.
- **Scenarios:** opening-001 through opening-004
- **Scoring:** Correct formula + warm delivery + silence

#### 4. Mastery Criteria

- **Score threshold:** >= 65 on 3 consecutive attempts
- **Tone:** Warm/encouraging detected (not FM-DJ clinical) in all 3
- **Silence minimum:** >= 3 seconds in 2 of 3
- **Context judgment:** >= 75% accuracy on when to use Label 7 vs not (MCR exercise)
- **What "done" looks like:** User delivers Label 7 with warmth and genuine interest, holds silence to give the other person the floor, and can distinguish appropriate vs inappropriate contexts.
- **Unlocks:** L7 (Value Labels)

#### 5. Integration Notes

- **Common sequence post-Label 7:** Label 7 -> They begin talking -> Mirror their opening statement -> Label 1-3 (affect shift in their opening) -> Continue with N9 sequence.
- **Pairs with Mirrors:** After Label 7, the counterpart's first statement is prime Mirroring material. Mirror their opening words to show you are listening.
- **Cultural note:** Some cultures expect pleasantries before business. Label 7 skips this. The research says "most people are relieved" and "those who want chitchat circle back pleasantly." The app should note this cultural consideration.

### LESSON L7: Value Labels -- "Value Is Not There" (Label 8)

#### 1. Concept Introduction

**What:** When someone says "the price is too high," they are talking about price. The Value Label redirects to the actual issue: value. "It seems like the value is just not there for you." This is often a deliberate Mis-Label -- their correction reveals what would make the deal work.

**Why:** Price objections are almost never about price. They are about: (1) not seeing sufficient value, (2) not being able to justify the expense to someone else, (3) feeling like they could get a better deal, or (4) testing whether you will fold. The Value Label bypasses price-focused haggling and moves to exploration of what value means to them. MI's amplified reflections -- deliberately overstating to evoke correction -- show d=0.14-0.28 for sustain-to-change talk shift (PMC5361059). Label 8 is a textbook amplified reflection: you overstate ("value isn't there") and the counterpart corrects toward what they actually need, producing Reason/Commitment-category change talk.

**When:** When someone objects to price, cost, investment, effort, or any form of "this costs too much." Also applicable when someone says "I can't afford this" or "this isn't in the budget."

**Source:** Black Swan Group Go-To Label 8: "It seems like the value is just not there for you." Voss notes this is "often a Mis-Label, and their correction will guide you on how to make a better deal."

#### 2. Core Mechanics

**Exact Verbal Formula:**
- "It seems like the value is just not there for you."
- Variations: "It sounds like you haven't seen enough value to justify this." / "It looks like the value proposition hasn't landed yet."

**Mis-Label Mechanics (Label 8 is designed as a Mis-Label):**
- The Label is deliberately slightly wrong -- they probably DO see SOME value, just not enough
- Their correction reveals WHAT they value: "No, it's not that. I just need to see [X]."
- The correction = information you did not have. Now you can address the real constraint.
- If they simply say "Yes" or "That's correct" -- the value genuinely is not there. Pivot to Label 6 (impasse).

**Decision Tree:**
```
Price objection -> Label 8: "It seems like the value is just not there."
  |
  |-> They CORRECT: "No, I see the value, but [real issue]."
  |     SUCCESS -- now address the real issue
  |
  |-> They AGREE: "Yes, that's correct."
  |     Value is genuinely missing. Pivot to Label 6 (impasse) or explore what WOULD create value
  |
  |-> They ELABORATE: "Well, it's more that..."
        PARTIAL -- they are opening up. Mirror their elaboration, then Label deeper.
```

**Delivery Requirements:**
- **Inflection:** Downward preferred. This is a statement of observation, not an accusation.
- **Alternative:** Short hesitation after "It seems like," then curious upward on "for you?" Acceptable but downward is stronger.
- **Pace:** FM-DJ voice. 110-140 WPM.
- **Silence:** 4+ seconds. The counterpart needs time to process whether they agree or want to correct.
- **Tone:** Neutral-observant. Not sympathetic (that sounds pitying). Not dismissive (that sounds like you don't care about their concern).

**Common Mistakes:**

| Mistake | Example | Correction |
|---------|---------|------------|
| Arguing about price | "But it's actually a great deal because..." | "It seems like the value is just not there for you." [SILENCE] |
| Making a concession | "Okay, I can lower the price." | Label first. The correction tells you what to do. |
| Labeling price directly | "It sounds like the price is the problem." | Price is never the issue. Value is. Label value. |
| Apologizing for price | "I know it's expensive, but..." | No apology. Observe: "It seems like the value isn't clear yet." |

#### 3. Practice Exercises

**Exercise 1: Price-to-Value Rewrite (Rewrite Text)**
- **Task:** Given a price-focused response, rewrite as a Value Label.
- **Examples:**
  - "I can give you 10% off." -> Rewrite as Value Label
  - "It's actually very competitive pricing." -> Rewrite as Value Label
  - "Let me check what discount I can get." -> Rewrite as Value Label
- **Scoring:** Does the rewrite redirect to VALUE (not price)? Contains "value" or equivalent concept? Correct syntax?

**Exercise 2: Correction Harvesting (Prompt-Response Audio)**
- **Task:** Deliver Label 8 to a scenario. AI counterpart responds with a correction. User must IDENTIFY what the correction revealed.
- **Scenarios:** value-001 through value-004 (new scenarios from Part 3)
- **Scoring:** Label delivery quality + can the user articulate what the correction revealed? (Text input after AI response)
- **Feedback:** "The counterpart said '[correction].' This reveals their actual concern is [X]. Now you know what to address."

**Exercise 3: Label 8 -> Label 6 Pivot (Audio-Spoken Delivery)**
- **Task:** Deliver Label 8. AI responds with agreement ("Yes, the value isn't there."). User must pivot to Label 6 or explore what would create value.
- **Scenarios:** value-003, value-004 (harder scenarios where value IS genuinely missing)
- **Scoring:** Correct Label 8 delivery + correct pivot recognition + pivot delivery quality

#### 4. Mastery Criteria

- **Score threshold:** >= 70 on 3 consecutive attempts
- **Downward inflection:** Confirmed in at least 2 of 3
- **Silence minimum:** >= 4 seconds in 2 of 3
- **Correction identification:** Can articulate what the counterpart's correction revealed in 2 of 3
- **Mis-Label awareness:** Can identify when Label 8 functions as a Mis-Label (MCR or verbal explanation)
- **What "done" looks like:** User delivers Value Labels with downward inflection, holds silence, recognizes corrections as information sources, and can pivot to Label 6 when value is genuinely absent.
- **Unlocks:** L8 (Impasse Labels)

#### 5. Integration Notes

- **Label 8 introduces Mis-Label concept:** This is the first lesson where the user encounters the idea that being "wrong" on purpose can be more valuable than being right. Seeds L9 (Mis-Labels).
- **Sequence after Label 8 correction:** They correct -> Mirror the correction -> Label 1-3 (the emotion behind the real concern) -> Calibrated Question ("How can we make this work?")
- **If they agree (value isn't there):** Pivot to Label 6 (impasse) OR "What would make the value clear?" (Calibrated Question). The pivot to Label 6 requires L8 mastery, which is why L7 is a prerequisite for L8.

### LESSON L8: Impasse Labels -- "Nothing I Can Say" (Label 6)

#### 1. Concept Introduction

**What:** The Impasse Label is a last-resort tool used when all other labeling, summarizing, and rapport-building has failed to produce movement. It sounds like surrender but functions as a Mis-Label: "It sounds like there is nothing I can say to get you to change your mind."

**Why:** This Label works in two ways: (1) If there IS a way forward, the counterpart will tell you what it is -- they correct the Mis-Label and reveal the path. (2) If there truly is no way forward, they say "That's right," and you end on the best possible relationship terms. Either outcome is a win. MI research provides the evidence for why timing matters: premature confrontation causes sustain talk to increase 20-30% (NCBI NBK571068), and discord mediates MI failure (path beta=-0.15 to -0.28 on outcomes, PMC5958907). Label 6 deployed too early IS premature confrontation -- the "coming alongside" reflection (MI's repair strategy) only works after thorough rapport-building.

**When:** ONLY after maximum effort. This is late-stage, "jaws of defeat" territory. Prerequisites: thorough labeling throughout the conversation, summarizing their position, building rapport. Using Label 6 too early is harmful -- it signals premature surrender and kills momentum.

**Source:** Black Swan Group Go-To Label 6: "It sounds like there is nothing I can say to get you to change your mind." Voss: "This effectively becomes a Mis-Label -- if there is any way to work out the deal, they will tell you."

#### 2. Core Mechanics

**Exact Verbal Formula:**
- "It sounds like there is nothing I can say to get you to change your mind."
- Variations: "It seems like we've reached a point where there's no path forward." / "It sounds like nothing I can do will make this work."

**Critical Deployment Timing:**
```
CORRECT deployment:
1. Thorough labeling (Labels 1-3, 4-5) throughout conversation -- CHECK
2. Summarized their position accurately -- CHECK
3. Built rapport and trust -- CHECK
4. Tried multiple approaches -- CHECK
5. Still stuck at impasse -- CHECK
-> THEN deploy Label 6

INCORRECT deployment:
1. First pushback from counterpart
-> WRONG. Too early. Use Labels 1-3, 4-5, or 8 first.

2. Mid-conversation frustration
-> WRONG. Continue labeling and summarizing.
```

**Mis-Label Mechanics:**
- Label 6 is DESIGNED as a Mis-Label (like Label 8)
- If there IS a way forward: they correct with "Actually, if you could [X], we could make this work."
- If there IS NO way forward: they confirm "That's right" and you part on best terms.

**Delivery Requirements:**
- **Inflection:** MUST be downward. Calm. Final. No curiosity -- this is acceptance.
- **Pace:** FM-DJ voice, slower end of range (110-120 WPM). Deliberate, measured.
- **Silence:** 5+ seconds MINIMUM. This is the most critical silence in the entire Label toolkit. The counterpart needs time to either find their way back to the table or confirm the impasse.
- **Tone:** Calm acceptance, not frustration. Not defeatism -- calm resignation with dignity. Inner voice: "I've done everything I can. Whatever they decide is okay."

**Common Mistakes:**

| Mistake | Example | Correction |
|---------|---------|------------|
| Using too early | Deploying Label 6 after first "no" | Exhaust Labels 1-5, 7, 8 before Label 6. MI research shows premature confrontation causes ST increase of 20-30% (NCBI NBK571068). Discord mediates MI failure (path beta=-0.15 to -0.28 on outcomes, PMC5958907). |
| Frustrated delivery | *exasperated* "It sounds like there's nothing I can say!" | Calm, downward, dignified. This is NOT a guilt trip. Frustrated tone triggers discord markers (YOU-statements, escalation r=0.31) that entrench the impasse rather than resolving it. |
| Upward inflection | "...to change your mind?" (sounds like a question) | MUST be downward. "...to change your mind." (period) |
| Breaking silence immediately | "...to change your mind. So what do you think?" | SILENCE. 5+ seconds minimum. Let it breathe. |
| Following up with argument | "...but I really think we should consider..." | NO follow-up argument. Silence only. Direct advice post-impasse causes ST amplification of 15-25% (resistance paradox). |

**Expert vs Novice Contrast:**

- **Novice:** *rushed, slightly irritated* "Look, it sounds like there's nothing I can say to change your mind, right? Because I've tried everything..."
- **Expert:** *calm, measured, downward inflection* "It sounds like there is nothing I can say to get you to change your mind." *[5+ seconds of silence]*

#### 3. Practice Exercises

**Exercise 1: Deployment Timing Recognition (Spot-the-Technique)**
- **Task:** Listen to 5 conversation excerpts at different stages. Identify which ones are appropriate for Label 6 deployment.
- **Scenarios:** Mix of early pushback (wrong), mid-conversation resistance (wrong), and genuine late-stage impasse (correct)
- **Scoring:** Correct identification of appropriate deployment moment (>= 4 of 5)
- **Feedback:** "Label 6 is a last resort. In scenario [X], you could still try [Label type Y] before reaching for the impasse Label."

**Exercise 2: Downward Inflection Lock (Audio-Spoken Delivery)**
- **Task:** Record Label 6 with strict downward inflection. System verifies pitch contour on final phrase.
- **Scenarios:** impasse-001 through impasse-004 (new scenarios from Part 3)
- **Scoring:** Downward inflection confirmed (pitch drops on "mind") + FM-DJ compliance + Silence >= 5 seconds
- **Feedback:** If upward: "Your pitch rose on 'mind' -- this turns it into a question and weakens the impact. Downward inflection signals calm acceptance."

**Exercise 3: Full Impasse Sequence (Prompt-Response Audio)**
- **Task:** Multi-turn: User Labels throughout a conversation, encounters impasse, deploys Label 6. AI responds with either correction (way forward) or confirmation (true impasse).
- **Scenarios:** impasse-002, impasse-004 (complex scenarios with multi-turn buildup)
- **Scoring:** Quality of pre-impasse labeling + Label 6 delivery + correct interpretation of AI response (did the user recognize whether the counterpart corrected or confirmed?)

#### 4. Mastery Criteria

- **Score threshold:** >= 75 on 3 consecutive attempts
- **Downward inflection:** MANDATORY in all 3 attempts. Any upward inflection = automatic fail.
- **Silence minimum:** >= 5 seconds in all 3 attempts
- **Deployment timing:** >= 80% accuracy on Spot-the-Technique exercise (knows when Label 6 is appropriate)
- **Response interpretation:** Can correctly identify correction vs confirmation in AI response
- **What "done" looks like:** User delivers Label 6 with calm downward inflection, holds 5+ seconds of silence, correctly identifies appropriate deployment moments, and can interpret the counterpart's response.
- **Unlocks:** L9 (Mis-Labels)

#### 5. Integration Notes

- **Label 6 is the final direct Label:** After mastering Labels 1-8, the user has the complete toolkit for direct labeling. L9 (Mis-Labels) adds the meta-technique layer.
- **"That's right" vs "You're right" matters most here:** When the counterpart confirms an impasse Label with "That's right," this is a genuine endpoint -- they feel understood, even if the deal didn't work. "You're right" would mean they are dismissing you.
- **Relationship preservation:** Label 6 is unique in that it prioritizes the RELATIONSHIP over the outcome. Even if the negotiation fails, ending with a well-delivered Label 6 preserves the relationship for future interactions.
- **Links to Value Labels:** If a Label 8 (value) produces confirmation ("Yes, the value isn't there"), the next move is often Label 6 (impasse). This L7 -> L8 sequence is why L7 is a prerequisite for L8.

### LESSON L9: Deliberate Mis-Labeling -- Unlocking Corrections (Advanced)

#### 1. Concept Introduction

**What:** Mis-Labels are Labels that are intentionally slightly wrong. The "wrongness" leverages the human "irresistible urge to correct" -- the counterpart corrects your observation and, in doing so, reveals their ACTUAL concern, constraint, or emotion. This is what Black Swan calls the "Black Swan Lie Detector."

**Why:** Direct labels ("It seems like you're worried about X") can only confirm what you already suspect. Mis-Labels unlock what you DON'T know. The correction is more valuable than the confirmation because it surfaces information the counterpart would not have volunteered on their own. MI's amplified reflection research confirms this mechanism: correction evokes change talk if a curious, deferential tone is maintained (d=0.14-0.28 sustain-to-change shift). However, wrong tone triggers discord -- over-amplification without empathy causes dropout risk to increase (OR=1.8-2.5) and sustain talk to entrench 15-25% (PMC5958907, NCBI NBK571068). Tone is the difference between a Mis-Label win and a backfire.

**When:** When you have a hypothesis but are not sure. When the conversation has stalled and you need new information. When you suspect the counterpart is withholding something. NEVER as a first move -- rapport and trust must be established through direct Labels first.

**Source:** Black Swan Group Mis-Label Mechanics. Labels 6 and 8 are designed as Mis-Labels by default. Any Label can become a Mis-Label intentionally or accidentally.

#### 2. Core Mechanics

**How Mis-Labeling Works:**
1. You have a hypothesis about their emotion/concern
2. You deliberately label something ADJACENT to that hypothesis -- slightly off
3. They feel the urge to correct: "No, that's not it. The real issue is..."
4. The correction = the Black Swan (hidden information)

**Example:**
- You suspect they are worried about budget, but you are not sure
- Mis-Label: "It seems like the timeline is the real concern here."
- Correction: "No, the timeline is fine. It's the budget approval process that's killing us."
- You now know the EXACT concern without having asked directly

**Designed Mis-Labels (Built into the Go-To Labels):**
- Label 6 (impasse): "Nothing I can say to change your mind" -- deliberately invites correction
- Label 8 (value): "Value isn't there for you" -- deliberately invites correction about what value IS there

**Creating Your Own Mis-Labels:**
- Take your best guess and shift it slightly: wrong emotion, wrong cause, wrong constraint
- Make it PLAUSIBLE but not quite right
- Must sound genuine -- not joking, not sarcastic, not testing

**Delivery Requirements:**
- **Tone:** Curious and deferential. NEVER sarcastic or joking (unless very low-stakes context).
- **Inflection:** Curious upward inflection preferred -- signals genuine uncertainty, invites correction
- **Pace:** FM-DJ voice, 110-140 WPM
- **Silence:** 4+ seconds. The correction needs space to emerge.
- **Inner voice:** "I genuinely believe this might be the issue, and I'm okay being wrong."

**Success vs Failure Indicators:**
| Outcome | Signal | Meaning |
|---------|--------|---------|
| SUCCESS | Counterpart corrects with new info | Mis-Label worked -- Black Swan unlocked |
| PARTIAL | Counterpart agrees but with hesitation | Close but not quite -- probe deeper |
| FAILURE | Counterpart agrees fully ("Yes.") | Mis-Label was accidentally accurate, OR they are stonewalling |
| BACKFIRE | Counterpart becomes defensive | Mis-Label was too far off, wrong tone, or no rapport foundation |

**Risks and Guardrails:**
1. **Overuse (5+ in a row):** Appears manipulative, unintelligent, or not listening. LIMIT to 1-2 per conversation.
2. **Wrong tone:** Sarcastic/joking in high-stakes = trust destruction.
3. **No rapport foundation:** Mis-Labels without prior trust = resentment, not correction.
4. **Accidental derailment:** Misreading the room and Mis-Labeling something sensitive.

**Common Mistakes:**

| Mistake | Example | Correction |
|---------|---------|------------|
| Sarcastic delivery | *smirking* "It seems like money is no object for you." | Genuine curiosity. If you can't say it sincerely, don't say it. Over-amplification without empathy triggers discord and increases dropout risk (OR=1.8-2.5, PMC5958907). Tone is the path variable: curious tone evokes CT; wrong tone triggers discord (path beta=-0.15 to -0.28 on outcomes). |
| Using before rapport | Opening with a Mis-Label | Direct Labels first. Mis-Labels require trust. Without prior rapport, Mis-Labels cause entrenchment -- ST amplifies 15-25% via resistance paradox (NCBI NBK571068). |
| Over-using | 5 Mis-Labels in one conversation | Maximum 1-2 per conversation. Mix with direct Labels. |
| Can't handle the correction | Getting defensive when they correct you | "That makes sense. Tell me more about [correction]." The MI "coming alongside" repair reflection softens discord (ST decreases, alliance repair). Mirror their concern + silence. |

#### 3. Practice Exercises

**Exercise 1: Correction Harvesting (Prompt-Response Audio)**
- **Task:** Given a scenario with a known underlying issue, deliver a Mis-Label that is deliberately adjacent to the real issue. AI responds with correction. User identifies what the correction revealed.
- **Scenarios:** mislabel-001 through mislabel-004 (new scenarios from Part 3)
- **Scoring:** Mis-Label quality (plausible but slightly off) + delivery tone (curious, not sarcastic) + silence + ability to articulate correction value
- **Feedback:** "Your Mis-Label was too accurate -- they confirmed instead of correcting. Try shifting your hypothesis: instead of labeling [X], try labeling [adjacent concept]."

**Exercise 2: Designed Mis-Labels vs Accidents (Spot-the-Technique)**
- **Task:** Listen to audio clips. Identify: (a) was the Label intended as a Mis-Label? (b) did the counterpart correct or confirm? (c) what information was gained?
- **Clips:** 5 clips mixing direct Labels, designed Mis-Labels, and accidental Mis-Labels
- **Scoring:** Correct identification of intent (50%) + correct identification of outcome (50%)

**Exercise 3: Mis-Label Rewrite (Rewrite Text)**
- **Task:** Given a direct Label and a scenario, rewrite as a plausible Mis-Label that would invite correction.
- **Examples:**
  - Direct: "It seems like you're worried about budget." -> Mis-Label targeting adjacent concern
  - Direct: "It sounds like trust is the issue." -> Mis-Label targeting adjacent concern
  - Direct: "It looks like the timeline is pressuring you." -> Mis-Label targeting adjacent concern
- **Scoring:** Is the Mis-Label plausible? Is it adjacent (not identical)? Would it invite correction?

**Exercise 4: Tone Calibration (Audio-Spoken Delivery)**
- **Task:** Record a Mis-Label three times: (a) sarcastic, (b) curious, (c) deferential. Compare playback. Identify which would work and which would backfire.
- **Scoring:** Can distinguish appropriate delivery. Curious/deferential versions score higher.

#### 4. Mastery Criteria

- **Score threshold:** >= 75 on 3 consecutive attempts
- **Correction value:** Can articulate what the counterpart's correction revealed in all 3 attempts
- **Tone:** Curious/deferential confirmed. Zero sarcasm or joking detected.
- **Plausibility:** Mis-Label is adjacent to the real issue (not identical, not wildly off)
- **Restraint:** Does not deploy more than 2 Mis-Labels in a multi-turn exercise
- **What "done" looks like:** User crafts plausible Mis-Labels that invite correction, delivers with genuine curiosity, harvests the correction for new information, and limits usage to avoid over-deployment.
- **Unlocks:** Advanced labeling drills, Simulation Studio scenarios with Mis-Label objectives

#### 5. Integration Notes

- **Mis-Labels are the bridge to Simulation Studio:** In the Simulation Studio, AI counterparts will respond dynamically to Labels. Mis-Labels produce richer AI responses because the correction mechanic is built into the state object pattern.
- **VCM diagnostic integration:** Comfort with Mis-Labels requires tolerance for being "wrong" -- this maps to VCM Gate 4 (Belief: "I can handle being wrong in front of someone") and Gate 7 (Recovery: "I can recover from a Mis-Label that landed badly").
- **Mastery marker:** A user who consistently harvests corrections from Mis-Labels and uses them to redirect conversations has achieved the highest level of labeling proficiency. This is the "fluid chaining (2-3/min)" marker from the research.

## PART 4: SCORING RUBRICS

### Universal Scoring Architecture

All labeling exercises use a dual-track scoring system:

1. **Text Analysis** (for transcript-based scoring): Syntax (0-40) + Depth (0-60) = 0-100 total
2. **Audio Analysis** (for delivery-based scoring): FM-DJ Compliance + Inflection + Dynamic Silence

Text scores are always calculated. Audio scores are calculated when audio is available.

### Text Analysis Rubric (All Lesson Types)

#### Syntax Scoring (0-40 points)

| Component | Points | What to Measure | Implementation |
|-----------|--------|-----------------|----------------|
| Correct opener | 15 | Starts with "It seems/sounds/looks like..." or short forms | Match against VALID_OPENERS constant |
| Avoids I-framing | 10 | No "I'm hearing," "I think you," "I feel like you," etc. | Match against INVALID_I_FRAMES constant |
| Avoids You-framing | 5 | Does not start with "You seem," "You're," "You sound" | Match against INVALID_YOU_FRAMES constant |
| Statement form | 10 | Does not end with "?" and does not contain "right?" | Check for question mark and "right?" pattern |

**Label-Type-Specific Syntax Adjustments:**

| Label Type | Additional Syntax Requirements |
|-----------|-------------------------------|
| Labels 4-5 (Behavior Probe) | Must contain "reason for" or equivalent probe structure (+5 bonus) |
| Label 7 (Opening) | Must address readiness/agenda ("place you want to start," "something on your mind") (+5 bonus) |
| Label 8 (Value) | Must reference value/worth/investment (NOT price/cost/money) (+5 bonus) |
| Label 6 (Impasse) | Must contain surrender structure ("nothing I can say/do") (+5 bonus) |
| Mis-Label | No additional syntax requirement -- scored on plausibility instead |

#### Depth Scoring (0-60 points)

| Score Range | Level | What It Looks Like | Keyword Matching |
|-------------|-------|--------------------|-----------------|
| 0-10 | No emotion detected | Generic observation, no emotional content | No matches in SURFACE_EMOTIONS or UNDERLYING_DRIVERS |
| 11-25 | Surface emotion only | Names obvious emotion: "frustrated," "worried," "stressed" | Match in SURFACE_EMOTIONS, no match in UNDERLYING_DRIVERS |
| 26-45 | Surface + Driver | Names emotion AND hints at driver: "frustrated about the precedent" | Match in both SURFACE_EMOTIONS and UNDERLYING_DRIVERS |
| 46-60 | Pure driver / Identity | Names driver without surface emotion: "concerned about how leadership will view this" | Match in UNDERLYING_DRIVERS, no match in SURFACE_EMOTIONS |

**Label-Type-Specific Depth Adjustments:**

| Label Type | Depth Focus |

#### Counterpart Response Scoring (L7-L9, based on MI Change Talk/Sustain Talk)

For L7+ lessons (Value Labels, Impasse Labels, Mis-Labels), label success is scored not only on delivery quality but on the COUNTERPART'S response. MI's change talk (CT) vs sustain talk (ST) taxonomy provides a validated framework for classifying response valence and depth. CT predicts behavior change outcomes (r=0.07-0.15); ST inversely correlates (r=0.06-0.16 for status quo persistence). A CT:ST ratio >1 flags proficiency.

**DARN-CAT Taxonomy (Counterpart Response Classification):**

| Category | Change Talk Examples | Sustain Talk Examples | Meta Effect |
|----------|---------------------|----------------------|-------------|
| Desire (D) | "I want to...", "I'd like to..." | "I enjoy...", "I don't want to quit" | CT: r=0.03 (ns); ST: r=0.10* |
| Ability (A) | "I can...", "I'm capable of..." | "I can't...", "It's impossible" | CT: r=-0.04 (ns); ST: r=0.06* |
| Reasons (R) | "Because [benefit]...", "To improve..." | "Because [excuse]..." | CT: r=-0.09*; ST: r=0.16** |
| Need (N) | "I need to...", "Must change" | "I have to keep...", "Can't avoid" | CT: ns; ST: r=0.05 |
| Commitment (C) | "I will...", "I'm going to..." | "I'll never...", "Not committing" | CT: ns |
| Activation/Taking Steps (A/T) | "Ready to...", "First step is..." | "Not ready", "No plan" | CT: ns |

*Source: PMC6310665, PMC4237697. * p<0.05, ** p<0.01 (addiction meta, k=16-21).*

**L7+ Counterpart Response Scoring (applied post-3s silence):**

| Response Type | Score | Action |
|---------------|-------|--------|
| CT-Reason/Commitment (>20% of words) | +3 (Worked!) | Advance: "That's right?" then Calibrated Question |
| CT-Desire/Ability | +1 | Elaborate: Mirror then Label |
| ST-Any (>50% of words) | -2 (Backfire) | Re-Label empathetically |
| Neutral/Minimal | 0 | Dynamic Silence + Mirror |
| Correction w/ CT | +4 (Mis-Label Win) | Harvest: Summary |

*MITI 4.0 benchmarks: Cultivating CT (global 4-5/5), Softening ST. Implementation note: Gemini can classify transcript snippets at 95% F1 feasibility with fine-tuning (casaa.unm.edu MITI 3.1/4.0).*

**Progression with CT/ST Metrics:**

| Lesson | Label Focus | MI Anchor | Success Metric |
|--------|-----------|-----------|----------------|
| L1-L3 | Surface/Driver | Simple/Complex Reflections | Delivery + Basic CT (Desire/Ability) |
| L4-L6 | Affect/Behavior/Opening | Complex/Amplified | CT:ST >0.5; Reason category increases |
| L7-L9 | Value/Impasse/Mis-Label | Amplified/Double-Sided | CT-Commitment 10%+; Correction produces CT |

*Causal evidence path: Reflections -> CT -> outcomes (path r=0.20-0.40). Source: PMC5958907.*

#### Discord Detection (L8-L9 Backfire Scoring)

For L8 (Impasse) and L9 (Mis-Labels), the scoring system must detect when a label causes discord -- an interpersonal rupture that entrenches opposition rather than opening it. MI research provides measurable markers and evidence-backed risk metrics.

**Discord Markers to Flag (post-Label ASR transcript analysis):**
- YOU-statements: "You don't get it," "You expect too much"
- I-don't statements: "I don't see the point," "I don't agree with any of this"
- Rising pitch in counterpart response (detectable via audio analysis)
- Reply latency <2 seconds (defensive/reactive, not reflective)

**Scoring:** Any discord marker detected = -2 points. System prompts repair: "Mirror concern + silence."

**Evidence for Backfire Severity:**

| Outcome | Evidence | Backfire Mechanism |
|---------|----------|-------------------|
| Escalation | Poor MI reflections lead to discord and ST dominance (r=0.31); YOU-statements signal rupture (ICC=0.87 detectible) | Defensiveness; alliance rupture |
| Disengagement | Discord increases dropout risk (OR=1.8-2.5) | Hopelessness; "fire alarm" ignored |
| Entrenchment | Confrontation triggers resistance paradox -- ST amplifies 15-25% | Cognitive dissonance avoidance |

*Meta: Discord mediates MI failure (path beta=-0.15 to -0.28 on outcomes). Source: PMC5958907, NCBI NBK571068.*

**Per-Lesson Discord Risk Anchors:**

| Lesson | Risk Anchor | Success Metric |
|--------|------------|----------------|
| L8 Impasse | Discord from premature probe | No YOU-statements in response; ST softens |
| L9 Mis-Label | Over-amplify without empathy | Correction produces CT (not discord markers) |

**Repair Strategy:** When discord is detected, the system recommends MI's "coming alongside" repair -- restate the counterpart's no-change position gently, then hold silence. This softens discord (ST decreases, alliance repairs). Source: uwsp.edu MI+OARS Quick Guide.
