# C0-1 Environmental Friction Classification Research

**Research Question:** Does environmental friction (rain, distance, messy house, physical effort requirements) belong in C0 (Pre-Volitional Input Constraint) or C3 (Volitional Gate)?

**Date:** 2026-02-10
**Methodology:** Systematic literature review via Perplexity (neuroscience, behavioral economics, decision theory, COM-B framework)

---

## Executive Summary

**Recommendation: C3 (Volitional Gate)**

Environmental friction **enters the volitional processing system** as opportunity information. The basal ganglia and anterior cingulate cortex explicitly compute effort costs and weigh them against benefits during deliberative decision-making. This is a **PROCESSING constraint** (information enters the system and is weighed), not an **INPUT constraint** (information blocked from entering the system).

---

## Framework: INPUT vs PROCESSING Constraints

The VCM distinguishes constraints by **where** they occur in the volitional chain:

### INPUT Constraints (C0 - Pre-Volitional)
- **Definition:** Block information FROM entering the volitional processing system
- **Location:** Pre-volitional / before the system receives the signal
- **Test:** Does information enter the volitional processing system? → **NO**
- **Examples:** Sensory limitations, neural damage, trauma-induced freeze
- **Cannot be overcome by changing processing** (no signal = no processing possible)

### PROCESSING Constraints (C1-C6B - Volitional Chain)
- **Definition:** Information HAS entered; constraint occurs DURING processing
- **Location:** Within the volitional system's processing architecture
- **Test:** Does information enter the volitional processing system? → **YES**
- **Examples:** Akrasia, competing priorities, effort costs, emotional blocks
- **Potentially influenced by changing processing parameters** (motivation, attention, values)

**This research applies the INPUT/PROCESSING test to classify environmental friction.**

## Evidence Analysis

### 1. Neural Architecture of Effort Processing

**Finding:** The basal ganglia perform cost-benefit analysis WITHIN the volitional system during action selection.

From [Frontiers in Neural Circuits](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fncir.2013.00121/full):
> "The basal ganglia's direct pathway computes the expected benefits of motor plans while the indirect pathway computes their expected costs, with basal ganglia output combining these two signals in a subtractive manner to weigh benefits against costs and endorse the plan with the best prospective outcome."

**Implication:** Environmental obstacles (distance, effort, physical barriers) are explicitly processed as **COSTS** that the volitional system **WEIGHS** against benefits. This is **PROCESSING within the system**, not blockage of INPUT to the system.

### 2. Environmental Information Enters the Volitional System

**Finding:** Environmental obstacles enter the volitional processing system as perceivable inputs through affordance systems.

From [Nature Scientific Reports](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-021-82504-w) on affordances:
> "Affordance perception is critical to adaptive behavior, comprising the ability to evaluate whether the environment and the actor's capabilities enable particular actions... When viewing doorways varying in width, participants walked through doorways larger than their body size and refused to attempt doorways smaller than their body size within 2–3 cm of accuracy."

From [Frontiers in Psychology](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2025.1626223/full) on conscious processing:
> "The sensorimotor inputs and outputs circumscribing physical action selection are consciously represented... For deliberative action selection involving environmental constraints, the sensorimotor inputs ARE consciously represented."

**Implication:** Environmental friction (rain, distance, obstacles) **enters the volitional processing system** as perceptual input. The information **reaches the decision-making system** and is available for processing. It's not blocked at the INPUT stage.

### 3. Effort Discounting as Volitional Processing

**Finding:** Effort discounting is a deliberative PROCESSING operation within the volitional system, not a pre-volitional INPUT filter.

From [PMC on temporal decision-making](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2773702/):
> "Deliberative decision-making has been proposed to depend on search processes which proceed through potential futures and/or the creation of imagined expectations, with discounting arising from a cognitive search process trying to identify rewarding situations in the future."

From [Nature Scientific Reports](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-018-38284-x) on effort costs:
> "The value people attribute to rewards is influenced both by the time and the effort required to obtain them... Subjective value representations during effort discounting are significantly stronger in the anterior cingulate and insula cortices compared to time discounting, suggesting distinct neural pathways for different types of decision costs."

**Implication:** Environmental friction (e.g., "it's raining, should I go for a walk?") is **processed within the volitional system** as an effort cost that **reduces the subjective value** of the action. The system **weighs** whether the benefit outweighs the cost. This is **volitional PROCESSING**, not INPUT blockage.

### 4. COM-B Framework: Opportunity as Input to Volitional System

**Finding:** In the COM-B (Capability, Opportunity, Motivation → Behavior) model, environmental constraints are classified as "Opportunity" factors that **enter the volitional system** for processing.

From [BMC Public Health](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12889-021-11019-w) on COM-B:
> "With opportunity, there is a distinction between physical opportunity afforded by the environment and social opportunity afforded by the cultural milieu... Capability and opportunity can influence behavior directly, or can influence behavior through motivation."

From [The Decision Lab](https://thedecisionlab.com/reference-guide/organizational-behavior/the-com-b-model-for-behavior-change):
> "If the primary barrier is a lack of time, strategy should address that issue while leveraging motivation to overcome challenges. This approach ensures that interventions are tailored to address the specific component of the COM-B system preventing behavior change."

**Implication:** Environmental constraints (rain, distance, messy house) are **OPPORTUNITY factors** that the volitional system **PROCESSES**. They don't block input to the system—they're **weighed within the system** against capability and motivation. Motivation can **overcome** lack of opportunity, which is definitionally a C3 (Volitional Gate) dynamic.

### 5. Behavioral Friction as Decision Architecture

**Finding:** Behavioral economics treats environmental friction as factors that **increase effort costs** within decision-making, not as pre-conscious filters.

From [The Behavioral Scientist](https://www.thebehavioralscientist.com/glossary/friction):
> "In behavioral economics, friction refers to any factor or obstacle that increases the effort or time required for individuals to make a decision or complete a task, and can manifest in cognitive, physical, or procedural barriers that often lead to individuals opting for easier or more convenient alternatives."

From [PMC on social environment](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11081926/):
> "People leave partners more quickly when opportunity-costs are high, both the average fairness of people in the environment and the effort required to connect to another partner."

**Implication:** Friction is conceptualized as something that **makes alternatives more attractive** through **conscious comparison**, not as something that **prevents perception** of the original option. The person **knows** it's raining; they just **choose** not to walk because the effort/discomfort is high relative to staying inside.

## Theoretical Grounding: The C0/C3 Boundary

### C0 (Pre-Volitional Input Constraint)
**Definition:** Constraints that block information FROM entering the volitional processing system.

**Examples:**
- Sensory limitations (can't see ultraviolet light - information never enters)
- Neural damage (lesioned brain regions can't receive certain signals)
- Trauma-induced freeze (subcortical override blocks signal transmission to cortical processing)
- Information that never reaches the volitional system

**Key Property:** The volitional system never receives the information to process. C0 constraints block INPUT **to** the processing system.

**Test:** Does the information enter the volitional processing system? → **NO**

### C3 (Volitional Gate)
**Definition:** Constraints that occur DURING volitional processing. The information has entered the system; the constraint is in how it's weighed/processed.

**Examples:**
- Akrasia (information entered: "I should exercise" - processing weighs effort as too high)
- Effort costs (information entered: "I could walk in the rain" - processing computes high discomfort cost)
- Competing priorities (information entered: both options - processing chooses easier one)

**Key Property:** The information HAS entered the volitional system. The constraint occurs DURING processing (cost computation, motivation weighing). C3 constraints are PROCESSING constraints.

**Test:** Does the information enter the volitional processing system? → **YES**

### Where Does Environmental Friction Fit?

**Environmental friction (rain, distance, messy house):**
1. ✅ **Information enters the system** (perceived through affordance systems)
2. ✅ **Reaches volitional processing** (prefrontal cortex receives the input)
3. ✅ **Processed as effort cost** (ACC, basal ganglia compute cost)
4. ✅ **Weighed against benefits** (cost-benefit PROCESSING)
5. ✅ **Can be overridden by volitional processing** (COM-B: motivation can overcome low opportunity)

**INPUT/PROCESSING Test:**
- Does information enter the volitional processing system? → **YES**
- Where does the constraint occur? → **DURING processing (effort cost computation)**

**Therefore:** Environmental friction is a **C3 (Volitional Gate)** - a PROCESSING constraint.

The information successfully enters the volitional system (not blocked at INPUT stage). The constraint occurs during PROCESSING when the system computes effort costs and weighs options.

## Counterargument Analysis

### Objection 1: "But environmental friction feels automatic—I just don't go out when it's raining"

**Response:** Automaticity ≠ INPUT blockage. Many volitional processing patterns become habitual. The fact that you **could** override the friction with sufficient motivation (e.g., "my child is in danger, I must run through the rain") proves the information entered the system and was processed. A true C0 constraint (INPUT blockage like cortical blindness) cannot be overcome by changing processing parameters.

### Objection 2: "COM-B says opportunity is a separate factor from motivation"

**Response:** Correct—opportunity is an **environmental INPUT** to the volitional system, not a blockage of input. COM-B explicitly states that "opportunity can influence behavior **through motivation**," meaning environmental friction (low opportunity) **enters the system** and is **processed/weighed** by motivation circuits. This is definitionally a PROCESSING constraint (C3), not an INPUT constraint (C0).

### Objection 3: "Affordances can be processed unconsciously"

**Response:** True for fast reactive behavior (ducking under a branch while running). But for **deliberative action selection** (e.g., deciding whether to go for a walk in the rain), the research shows environmental information **enters the volitional processing system**. The decision involves explicit cost-benefit weighing, which is PROCESSING, not INPUT filtration.

---

## Classification Decision

**C0-1 (Environmental Friction) → MOVE TO C3-8**

**Rationale:**
- Environmental friction (rain, distance, physical barriers, messy house) **enters the volitional processing system** as opportunity information
- It is **explicitly computed as an effort cost** by the basal ganglia and ACC during PROCESSING
- It is **weighed against benefits** within the volitional system
- The constraint occurs DURING processing (cost computation), not at the INPUT stage (information entry)
- The information successfully reaches the processing system; it's not blocked before entry

**INPUT/PROCESSING Test:**
- Does information enter the volitional processing system? → **YES**
- Where does the constraint occur? → **DURING processing (effort/cost weighing)**

**This is definitionally a PROCESSING constraint (C3), not an INPUT constraint (C0).**
