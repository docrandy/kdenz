# VCM Framework: C0/C3 Boundary Refinement Recommendations

**Research Date:** 2026-02-10
**Purpose:** Apply philosophical research to refine VCM C0/C3 boundary definitions and classification protocols

---

## Executive Summary

Philosophical research **strongly validates** the VCM distinction between C0 (Environmental Friction) and C3 (Heteronomous Action). However, it reveals three critical refinements needed:

1. **The boundary is not about external vs internal, but about will-formation vs execution**
2. **Internalized constraints can migrate from C3 to C0 through autonomous endorsement**
3. **Trauma responses may need a separate category (C3-derived states)**

**Key recommendation:** Replace simple "external agent" test with **5-criterion assessment protocol** to improve classification accuracy.

---

## Current VCM Definitions (From Claims Document)

### C0: Environmental Friction (Current Definition)

**"Obstacles that make goal-directed action harder but do not compromise the agent's autonomous will."**

Examples: Locked doors, lack of money, physical fatigue, cold weather

**Philosophical assessment:** STRONG foundation, but needs precision about "autonomous will"

---

### C3: Heteronomous Action (Current Definition)

**"Situations where the agent acts according to external demands rather than their own autonomous will."**

Examples: Boss commands, social coercion, authority demands

**Philosophical assessment:** CORRECT core intuition, but needs clarification on what "external demands" means

## Philosophical Refinements

### Refinement 1: Define "Autonomous Will" Precisely

**Current ambiguity:** What counts as "autonomous will"?

**Philosophical clarification:**
- **Autonomous will = Acting from second-order volitions**
  - Agent identifies with their first-order desires
  - Agent willingly endorses their action
  - Agent is reasons-responsive

- **Heteronomous will = Acting against second-order volitions**
  - External pressure creates first-order desires
  - Agent does NOT endorse these desires
  - Reasons-responsiveness is compromised

**Recommended definition update:**

**C0 (Environmental Friction):** Obstacles that make execution difficult but preserve the agent's **second-order volitions** and **reasons-responsiveness**. The agent remains the author of their will, even if execution is constrained.

**C3 (Heteronomous Action):** Situations where external agents impose demands that **compromise second-order volitions** and **reasons-responsiveness**. The agent's will is shaped by external control rather than their own deliberation.

---

### Refinement 2: Source Is Necessary But Not Sufficient

**Current approach:** C3 requires "external demands"

**Philosophical insight:** Not all external demands are C3, and not all C0 lacks external origin.

**Key distinctions:**

**External demands that are C0 (internalized):**
- Professional norms the agent identifies with
- Cultural values the agent genuinely endorses
- Social roles the agent willingly embraces
- **Test:** Agent would maintain these even if external pressure disappeared

**Internal states that are C3-derived:**
- Trauma responses (freeze, fawn)
- Internalized oppression
- Anxiety from past coercion
- **Test:** Originated from external control, still compromises will

**Recommended classification protocol:**

**Step 1:** Identify source
- Another agent with control intent? → Candidate for C3
- Natural/circumstantial? → Candidate for C0

**Step 2:** Assess current relationship (critical addition)
- Does agent willingly endorse this? → May be C0 even if externally originated
- Does agent feel compelled? → May be C3 even if no current external agent

**Conclusion:** Source is an **initial filter**, not the **final determination**

---

### Refinement 3: Add Reasons-Responsiveness Test

**Current approach:** Focuses on source (external demands)

**Missing element:** Whether the constraint compromises deliberation

**Philosophical insight:** The critical question is whether the agent can still **recognize and respond to reasons**.

**Recommended addition:**

**C0 criteria include:**
- ✓ Agent can deliberate about alternatives
- ✓ Agent would respond if reasons changed
- ✓ Obstacle affects execution, not deliberation

**C3 criteria include:**
- ✓ Deliberation is bypassed or overridden
- ✓ External pressure dominates regardless of reasons
- ✓ Compliance mechanism supersedes reason-tracking

**Practical test:** "If the reasons for/against this action changed significantly, would you respond differently?"
- YES → C0 (reasons-responsive)
- NO → C3 (reasons-responsiveness compromised)

**Example applications:**

**Locked door (C0):**
- Q: "If you learned there's a window you could use, would you try it?"
- A: "Yes, of course"
- **Result:** Reasons-responsive → C0 confirmed

**Boss threat (C3):**
- Q: "If you learned that working this weekend would actually harm the project, would you refuse?"
- A: "I'd want to, but I can't risk getting fired"
- **Result:** Reasons-responsiveness compromised by threat → C3 confirmed

---

### Refinement 4: Distinguish Autonomy Experience

**Current approach:** Implicit in "autonomous will" but not operationalized

**Missing element:** Phenomenological test for controlled vs autonomous motivation

**Philosophical insight (SDT):** Same behavior can be autonomous or controlled depending on how it's experienced.

**Recommended addition:**

**C0 phenomenology:**
- "This is difficult, but it's my choice"
- "I'm choosing this despite the obstacles"
- Internal locus of causality
- Willingly endorsing

**C3 phenomenology:**
- "I have to do this" / "I have no choice"
- "I'm only doing this because of [external pressure]"
- External locus of causality
- Feeling compelled

**Practical test:** "Do you feel you're choosing this, or do you feel you have to?"
- Choosing → C0
- Have to → C3

**Important:** This is a **supporting criterion**, not sole determinant (risk of rationalization)

## Revised Classification Protocol

### The 5-Criterion Algorithm for C0/C3 Boundary

**Criterion 1: SOURCE**
- Is this imposed by another agent with control intent?
  - NO → Points to C0
  - YES → Points to C3

**Criterion 2: MECHANISM**
- Does this affect execution (first-order) or will-formation (second-order)?
  - First-order → Points to C0
  - Second-order → Points to C3

**Criterion 3: REASONS-RESPONSIVENESS**
- Can agent recognize and respond to reasons?
  - YES → Points to C0
  - NO → Points to C3

**Criterion 4: AUTONOMY EXPERIENCE**
- Does agent willingly endorse or feel compelled?
  - Willingly endorses → Points to C0
  - Feels compelled → Points to C3

**Criterion 5: WILL-OVERBEARING**
- Does this make options less attractive or override will?
  - Less attractive → Points to C0
  - Override will → Points to C3

**Decision Rule:**
- **4-5 criteria agree:** Strong classification
- **3 criteria agree:** Moderate confidence classification
- **2-2-1 split:** AMBIGUOUS, requires context analysis

## Application to VCM Scenarios

### Scenario Analysis: Boss Commands

**Current VCM classification:** C3 (Heteronomous Action)

**Philosophical validation:**

**Criterion 1 (Source):** YES - boss with control intent → C3
**Criterion 2 (Mechanism):** Second-order - creates unwanted desire to comply → C3
**Criterion 3 (Reasons-responsive):** NO - compliance bypasses deliberation → C3
**Criterion 4 (Autonomy):** Feels compelled, not willing → C3
**Criterion 5 (Will-overbearing):** YES - "do this or fired" overrides will → C3

**Result: 5/5 criteria = STRONG C3**

**VCM classification confirmed:** CORRECT

---

### Scenario Analysis: Social Pressure (Mild)

**Current VCM classification:** Ambiguous (sometimes C0, sometimes C3)

**Philosophical analysis:** DEPENDS on intensity and agent's response

**Mild social pressure example:** Friend expresses disappointment

**Criterion 1 (Source):** Weak YES - friend's reaction, but not control intent → C0/C3 boundary
**Criterion 2 (Mechanism):** First-order - makes compliance more attractive but doesn't override values → C0
**Criterion 3 (Reasons-responsive):** YES - agent can deliberate and choose → C0
**Criterion 4 (Autonomy):** Probably willing (uncomfortable but choosing) → C0
**Criterion 5 (Will-overbearing):** NO - just makes option less attractive → C0

**Result: 4/5 criteria = STRONG C0**

**Strong social pressure example:** Friend threatens to end friendship

**Criterion 1 (Source):** YES - friend with control intent → C3
**Criterion 2 (Mechanism):** Second-order - threatens core need (relatedness) → C3
**Criterion 3 (Reasons-responsive):** Compromised - fear overrides deliberation → C3
**Criterion 4 (Autonomy):** Feels compelled by threat → C3
**Criterion 5 (Will-overbearing):** YES - relationship threat is severe → C3

**Result: 5/5 criteria = STRONG C3**

**Recommendation:** VCM should distinguish **mild social influence (C0)** from **strong social coercion (C3)** based on intensity and will-overbearing.

---

### Scenario Analysis: Internalized Professional Norms

**Current VCM classification:** Not explicitly addressed

**Example:** "I need to dress professionally for work"

**Philosophical analysis:** DEPENDS on internalization

**Case A: Fully internalized**

**Criterion 1 (Source):** Originally external, now internalized → C0
**Criterion 2 (Mechanism):** First-order - aligns with agent's values → C0
**Criterion 3 (Reasons-responsive):** YES - agent deliberates about professional context → C0
**Criterion 4 (Autonomy):** Willingly endorses ("I value professionalism") → C0
**Criterion 5 (Will-overbearing):** NO - agent chooses based on own values → C0

**Result: 5/5 criteria = STRONG C0**

**Case B: Still external pressure**

**Criterion 1 (Source):** Current external enforcement (boss will criticize) → C3
**Criterion 2 (Mechanism):** Second-order - conflicts with agent's values → C3
**Criterion 3 (Reasons-responsive):** Compromised - fear of judgment overrides → C3
**Criterion 4 (Autonomy):** Feels compelled ("I have to or they'll judge") → C3
**Criterion 5 (Will-overbearing):** YES - social/professional consequences override → C3

**Result: 5/5 criteria = STRONG C3**

**Recommendation:** VCM should assess **current relationship** to norms, not just origin. Test: "If workplace norms changed tomorrow, would you keep this practice?"
- YES → Internalized, C0
- NO → Still external pressure, C3

---

### Scenario Analysis: Trauma Response (Freeze)

**Current VCM classification:** Not explicitly addressed

**Example:** Agent freezes when confronted with authority figure due to past trauma

**Philosophical analysis:** MIXED (neither pure C0 nor pure C3)

**Criterion 1 (Source):** Past trauma (C3 origin), no current external agent → MIXED
**Criterion 2 (Mechanism):** Second-order - agent doesn't want to freeze → C3
**Criterion 3 (Reasons-responsive):** NO - freeze bypasses deliberation → C3
**Criterion 4 (Autonomy):** Not willingly endorsed → C3
**Criterion 5 (Will-overbearing):** YES - freeze overrides will → C3

**Result: 1 C0, 4 C3, but source is ambiguous**

**Philosophical problem:**
- Not C0 (affects second-order volitions, compromises reasons-responsiveness)
- Not C3 (no current external agent controlling behavior)
- **Originated in C3** (trauma was external control) but now **triggered by circumstance**

**Recommendation:** VCM should consider **C3-DERIVED category** for states that:
- Originated in heteronomous action (trauma, conditioning)
- Are triggered by current circumstances (no active external control)
- Still compromise will-formation and reasons-responsiveness

**Alternative:** Classify trauma responses as **C3** with notation "trauma-derived" to distinguish from active coercion.

---

### Scenario Analysis: Poverty / Resource Constraints

**Current VCM classification:** C0 (Environmental Friction)

**Example:** Agent can't afford food

**Philosophical analysis:** Mostly C0, with caveat about systemic issues

**Criterion 1 (Source):** Circumstantial (immediate situation) → C0
**Criterion 2 (Mechanism):** First-order - limits options, doesn't change values → C0
**Criterion 3 (Reasons-responsive):** YES - agent deliberates about trade-offs → C0
**Criterion 4 (Autonomy):** Willingly chooses given constraints → C0
**Criterion 5 (Will-overbearing):** NO - difficult choice, but still genuine deliberation → C0

**Result: 5/5 criteria = STRONG C0**

**Philosophical caveat:** Critical theorists would argue systemic poverty IS agent-imposed (structural oppression) and should be C3.

**VCM recommendation:**
- **Immediate resource constraints:** Classify as C0 for individual agency analysis
- **Systemic oppression:** Acknowledge philosophical debate, but VCM focuses on individual will
- **Note:** If poverty is result of specific coercion (debt slavery, economic abuse), consider C3

## Special Categories and Edge Cases

### Category 1: Internalized Constraints (C3 → C0 migration)

**Definition:** Constraints that originated as external control (C3) but have been internalized and are now autonomously endorsed (C0).

**Identification criteria:**
- Originally imposed by external agent
- Agent now identifies with the constraint
- Agent would maintain it even if external pressure disappeared
- Passes autonomy experience test (willingly endorsing)

**Examples:**
- Professional standards agent genuinely values
- Cultural practices agent has adopted as their own
- Moral norms agent has internalized through reflection

**VCM classification:** C0 (current state, not origin)

**Rationale:** Philosophical frameworks agree that internalized norms that are autonomously endorsed don't compromise agency.

---

### Category 2: C3-Derived States (trauma, conditioning)

**Definition:** Psychological states that originated in heteronomous action but are triggered by current circumstances without active external control.

**Identification criteria:**
- Clear historical origin in coercion/trauma (C3)
- No current external agent actively controlling
- Still compromises second-order volitions and reasons-responsiveness
- Agent doesn't willingly endorse the response

**Examples:**
- Trauma freeze response
- Conditioned fear from past abuse
- Learned helplessness from past coercion

**VCM classification:** RECOMMEND separate category "C3-derived" OR classify as C3 with "trauma-derived" notation

**Rationale:** These don't fit cleanly in C0 (affect will-formation) or C3 (no current external control), but clearly compromise autonomy.

**Philosophical support:**
- Frankfurt: Affects second-order volitions (not endorsed)
- Reasons-responsiveness: Compromised (automatic response)
- SDT: Not autonomous motivation

---

### Category 3: Structural/Systemic Constraints

**Definition:** Background social structures that shape opportunities without specific agent control in the moment.

**Identification criteria:**
- No specific agent in immediate situation
- Systemic pattern (institutional, economic, social)
- Shapes options available to agent
- May or may not compromise autonomy

**Examples:**
- Economic inequality (poverty, unemployment)
- Institutional discrimination
- Social norms (not enforced in moment)

**VCM classification:** CONTEXT-DEPENDENT

**If agent maintains autonomy despite structure:** C0
- Still reasons-responsive
- Willingly endorses choices given constraints
- Structure affects options, not will-formation

**If structure compromises autonomy:** Consider C3
- Internalized oppression (agent has adopted oppressor's values)
- Ongoing enforcement (specific agents maintaining structure)
- Compromises reasons-responsiveness

**Recommendation:** VCM should:
1. Acknowledge philosophical debate about structural agency
2. Focus on individual will for practical assessment
3. Use 5-criterion algorithm to assess each case
4. Note systemic context when relevant

## Revised VCM Definitions (Recommended)

### C0: Environmental Friction (Revised Definition)

**"Obstacles that make goal-directed action more difficult but preserve the agent's second-order volitions and reasons-responsiveness. The agent remains the author of their will and willingly endorses their action despite execution constraints."**

**Key characteristics:**
- Source: Natural, circumstantial, impersonal, OR internalized and autonomously endorsed
- Mechanism: Affects execution difficulty, not will-formation
- Reasons-responsiveness: Preserved (agent can deliberate)
- Autonomy: Agent willingly endorses action
- Will: Not overbearing (makes options less attractive, doesn't override will)

**Examples:**
- Physical obstacles: Locked doors, broken equipment, terrain
- Resource constraints: Lack of money, time, energy
- Environmental conditions: Weather, darkness, noise
- Psychological friction: Anxiety, fatigue, mild discomfort
- Internalized norms: Professional standards agent genuinely values

---

### C3: Heteronomous Action (Revised Definition)

**"Situations where external agents impose demands that compromise the agent's second-order volitions and reasons-responsiveness. The agent's will is shaped by external control rather than their own deliberation, creating first-order desires the agent does not willingly endorse."**

**Key characteristics:**
- Source: Another agent with control intent (immediate or historical)
- Mechanism: Affects will-formation (second-order volitions)
- Reasons-responsiveness: Compromised (deliberation bypassed/overridden)
- Autonomy: Agent feels compelled, does not willingly endorse
- Will: Overbearing (overrides agent's will, not just makes options less attractive)

**Examples:**
- Authority commands: Boss demands, police orders, parental commands (children)
- Coercive threats: "Do X or Y negative consequence"
- Strong social pressure: Ostracism threats, group coercion
- Trauma-derived responses: Freeze, fawn, conditioned fear (C3-derived subtype)

**Subtypes:**
- **Active C3:** Current external agent controlling (boss threat, police order)
- **C3-derived:** Historical external control, current trigger (trauma response)

---

### C3-Derived: Trauma and Conditioned Responses (New Subcategory)

**"Psychological responses that originated in heteronomous action (trauma, abuse, coercion) and are triggered by current circumstances. These responses compromise second-order volitions and reasons-responsiveness but lack a current external agent actively imposing control."**

**Key characteristics:**
- Source: Historical external control (trauma, abuse)
- Trigger: Current circumstances (authority figure, social situation)
- Mechanism: Affects will-formation (automatic response overrides deliberation)
- Reasons-responsiveness: Compromised (response bypasses deliberation)
- Autonomy: Not willingly endorsed by agent

**Examples:**
- Freeze response triggered by authority figures
- Fawn response to perceived social threat
- Conditioned fear from past abuse
- Learned helplessness from past coercion

**Classification rationale:** These don't fit cleanly in C0 (affect will, not execution) or active C3 (no current controller), but clearly compromise autonomy and originated in heteronomous action.

### Recommendation 2: Develop Assessment Protocol

**Action:** Create structured assessment tool based on 5-criterion algorithm.

**Components:**
1. **Source assessment:** "Is this constraint imposed by another agent with intent to control your behavior?"
2. **Mechanism assessment:** "When you consider this situation, does it affect what you can do (execution) or what you want to do (your values/goals)?"
3. **Reasons-responsiveness test:** "If the reasons for/against this action changed, would you respond differently?"
4. **Autonomy experience assessment:** "Do you feel you're choosing this, or do you feel you have to?"
5. **Will-overbearing test:** "Could you genuinely choose otherwise, or does this override your choice?"

**Scoring:** Each criterion points to C0 or C3; classify based on majority (3+ criteria).

**Priority:** HIGH (operationalize framework)

**Practical implication:** Need multi-criterion assessment that goes beyond simple source identification.

**Confidence level:** HIGH for core distinction, MEDIUM for classification protocol, noting some inherent ambiguity in boundary cases.
