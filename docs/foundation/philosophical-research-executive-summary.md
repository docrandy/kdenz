# Philosophical Research: Executive Summary

**Research Date:** 2026-02-10
**Research Question:** How do philosophical frameworks distinguish autonomous vs heteronomous action, and what criteria can refine the VCM C0/C3 boundary?

---

## Key Finding

Philosophical frameworks **strongly validate** the VCM distinction between Environmental Friction (C0) and Heteronomous Action (C3). The boundary is not about **external vs internal sources**, but about whether constraints **compromise will-formation and reasons-responsiveness**.

**Bottom line:** Authority commands and social pressure are philosophically distinct from environmental obstacles because they operate on **second-order volitions** (what you want to want), not just **first-order execution** (how hard it is to do what you want).

---

## Five Convergent Philosophical Frameworks

### 1. Frankfurt's Hierarchical Model (Compatibilism)

**Key insight:** Free will requires alignment between **first-order desires** (what you want to do) and **second-order volitions** (what you want to want).

**Application to C0/C3:**
- **C0:** Affects first-order execution only (locked door makes leaving hard, but you still want to leave)
- **C3:** Affects second-order volitions (boss threat creates desire to comply that you don't endorse)

**The willing vs unwilling addict:** Same behavior (taking drug), different autonomy status based on second-order volitions.

---

### 2. Self-Determination Theory (Psychological)

**Key insight:** Motivation exists on a spectrum from **controlled** (feeling compelled) to **autonomous** (willingly endorsing).

**Application to C0/C3:**
- **C0:** Autonomous motivation preserved despite difficulty
- **C3:** Controlled motivation (external pressure creates "I have to" rather than "I choose to")

**Critical discovery:** External constraints can be **internalized** and become autonomous if the agent genuinely endorses them.

---

### 3. Fischer & Ravizza's Reasons-Responsiveness (Compatibilism)

**Key insight:** Moral responsibility requires that the agent's mechanism of action be **responsive to reasons**.

**Application to C0/C3:**
- **C0:** Agent remains reasons-responsive (can deliberate about alternatives)
- **C3:** Reasons-responsiveness compromised (external pressure bypasses deliberation)

**Test:** "If the reasons changed, would you respond differently?" YES = C0, NO = C3

---

### 4. Berlin's Two Concepts of Liberty (Political Philosophy)

**Key insight:** Only restrictions imposed by **other people** affect political freedom. Natural obstacles don't.

**Application to C0/C3:**
- **C0:** Natural, circumstantial, or impersonal constraints
- **C3:** Human-imposed constraints with intent to control

**Support for VCM:** Environmental obstacles are categorically different from social control.

---

### 5. Coercion Theory (Analytic Philosophy)

**Key insight:** Coercion requires **intentional will-overbearing** by another agent, not just making options less attractive.

**Application to C0/C3:**
- **C0:** Makes options less attractive but doesn't override will
- **C3:** Overrides the will (creates options "no reasonable person could refuse")

**Frankfurt's strict criterion:** Coercion must arouse desire that **overbears** the victim's will.

---

## The Five-Criterion Classification Algorithm

All frameworks converge on these five tests:

### Criterion 1: SOURCE
- **C0:** Natural, circumstantial, impersonal
- **C3:** Another agent with control intent

### Criterion 2: MECHANISM
- **C0:** Affects first-order execution only
- **C3:** Affects second-order volitions (will-formation)

### Criterion 3: REASONS-RESPONSIVENESS
- **C0:** Agent can deliberate and respond to reasons
- **C3:** Deliberation bypassed or overridden

### Criterion 4: AUTONOMY EXPERIENCE
- **C0:** Agent willingly endorses action
- **C3:** Agent feels compelled, doesn't endorse

### Criterion 5: WILL-OVERBEARING
- **C0:** Makes options less attractive
- **C3:** Overrides the will

**Decision rule:** 3+ criteria agreement = classification. Mixed results = ambiguous case requiring context analysis.

## Three Critical Refinements for VCM

### Refinement 1: Internalized Constraints Can Migrate from C3 to C0

**Discovery:** A constraint can start as external control (C3) but become autonomous (C0) through internalization.

**Example:**
- **Initial:** "I must exercise because doctor demands it" → C3 (controlled motivation)
- **Internalized:** "I exercise because I value health" → C0 (autonomous motivation)

**Test:** "If the external pressure disappeared, would you still do this?"
- YES → Internalized, now C0
- NO → Still C3

**Implication:** VCM must assess **current relationship** to constraint, not just origin.

---

### Refinement 2: Source Is Necessary But Not Sufficient

**Discovery:** Not all external demands are C3, and not all C0 lacks external origin.

**Examples of external origins that are now C0:**
- Professional norms the agent genuinely values
- Cultural practices the agent has adopted as their own
- Social roles the agent willingly embraces

**Examples of internal states that are C3-derived:**
- Trauma responses (freeze, fawn)
- Conditioned fear from past abuse
- Internalized oppression

**Implication:** Source is an **initial filter**, not the **final determination**. Must assess all 5 criteria.

---

### Refinement 3: Trauma Responses May Need Separate Category

**Discovery:** Trauma responses don't fit cleanly in C0 or C3.

**Why not C0:**
- Affect second-order volitions (agent doesn't want to freeze)
- Compromise reasons-responsiveness (automatic response)
- Not willingly endorsed

**Why not C3:**
- No current external agent controlling
- Triggered by circumstance, not active coercion

**Philosophical analysis:** These are **C3-derived states** - originated in heteronomous action (trauma was external control) but triggered by current circumstances.

**Recommendation:** Add **C3-derived subcategory** for states that originated in external control but are triggered circumstantially.

## Validated VCM Distinctions

### CONFIRMED: Physical Obstacles Are C0

**Philosophical support:** Universal agreement across all frameworks
- Berlin: Natural obstacles don't affect political freedom
- Frankfurt: Don't affect second-order volitions
- Fischer & Ravizza: Don't compromise reasons-responsiveness
- SDT: Don't create controlled motivation

**Examples:** Locked door, broken elevator, impassable terrain

---

### CONFIRMED: Authority Commands Are C3

**Philosophical support:** Universal agreement across all frameworks
- Berlin: Human-imposed restrictions affect freedom
- Frankfurt: Create first-order desires that conflict with second-order volitions
- Fischer & Ravizza: Compromise reasons-responsiveness
- SDT: Create controlled motivation
- Coercion theory: Intentional will-overbearing by another agent

**Examples:** Boss demands, police orders, parental commands (to children)

---

### REFINED: Social Pressure Is Context-Dependent

**Discovery:** Social pressure exists on a spectrum from mild influence (C0) to strong coercion (C3).

**Mild social influence → C0:**
- Friend expresses disappointment
- Doesn't override will
- Agent remains reasons-responsive
- Uncomfortable but not controlling

**Strong social coercion → C3:**
- Ostracism threat
- Overrides will through fear of rejection
- Compromises reasons-responsiveness
- Agent feels compelled

**Test:** Apply 5-criterion algorithm to assess intensity and will-overbearing.

---

### REFINED: Internalized Norms Are Context-Dependent

**Discovery:** Same norm can be C0 or C3 depending on agent's relationship to it.

**Internalized and autonomous → C0:**
- Professional standards agent genuinely values
- Would maintain even if external pressure disappeared
- Willingly endorsed
- Aligns with second-order volitions

**Still external pressure → C3:**
- Professional standards agent resents
- Only maintains to avoid judgment
- Feels compelled ("I have to")
- Conflicts with second-order volitions

**Test:** "If workplace norms changed tomorrow, would you keep this practice?"
- YES → Internalized, C0
- NO → Still external, C3

## Boundary Cases Requiring Special Attention

### Boundary Case 1: Trauma Responses

**Status:** Neither pure C0 nor pure C3

**Recommendation:** Create **C3-derived subcategory** for responses that:
- Originated in heteronomous action (trauma, abuse)
- Are triggered by current circumstances
- Compromise will-formation despite no active external control

**Examples:** Freeze response to authority figures, fawn response to social threat

---

### Boundary Case 2: Internalized Oppression

**Status:** Appears C0 (internalized) but may mask C3 (oppression)

**Challenge:** Agent may report autonomous motivation while actually experiencing controlled motivation due to internalization of oppressor's values.

**Recommendation:**
- Assess all 5 criteria, not just self-report
- Weight objective criteria (reasons-responsiveness, mechanism) over autonomy experience if conflict
- Consider whether agent would maintain values if social structure changed

---

### Boundary Case 3: Systemic/Structural Constraints

**Status:** Philosophical debate about whether systems constitute "agents"

**Individualist view:** No specific agent in the moment → C0 (resource constraint)
**Structural view:** Systems ARE agents (collective agency) → C3 (systemic oppression)

**Recommendation:**
- VCM focuses on individual will, so default to C0 for background structural constraints
- Use 5-criterion algorithm for specific institutional demands
- Acknowledge philosophical debate in framework documentation

**Example:** Poverty is generally C0 (resource constraint), but if result of specific economic abuse, consider C3

---

## Revised VCM Definitions (Recommended)

### C0: Environmental Friction

**"Obstacles that make goal-directed action more difficult but preserve the agent's second-order volitions and reasons-responsiveness. The agent remains the author of their will and willingly endorses their action despite execution constraints."**

**Examples:**
- Physical obstacles (locked doors, terrain)
- Resource constraints (money, time, energy)
- Environmental conditions (weather, darkness)
- Psychological friction (anxiety, fatigue)
- **Internalized norms** (professional standards agent genuinely values)

---

### C3: Heteronomous Action

**"Situations where external agents impose demands that compromise the agent's second-order volitions and reasons-responsiveness. The agent's will is shaped by external control rather than their own deliberation, creating first-order desires the agent does not willingly endorse."**

**Examples:**
- Authority commands (boss, police, parent)
- Coercive threats ("do X or Y consequence")
- Strong social pressure (ostracism, group coercion)
- **C3-derived:** Trauma responses (freeze, fawn, conditioned fear)
