# C0/C3 Boundary: Philosophical Classification Criteria

**Research Date:** 2026-02-10
**Purpose:** Actionable philosophical criteria for distinguishing Environmental Friction (C0) from Heteronomous Action (C3)

---

## Executive Summary

Philosophical research reveals **five convergent criteria** for the C0/C3 boundary. All major frameworks (Frankfurt, SDT, Fischer & Ravizza, Berlin) agree on the core distinction: **C0 affects execution; C3 affects will-formation.**

**Key insight:** The question isn't "Is this external?" but rather "Does this compromise the agent's reasons-responsiveness and second-order volitions?"

---

## The Five Philosophical Criteria

### Criterion 1: Source of Constraint

**Question:** Is this constraint imposed by another agent with intent to control behavior?

**C0 (Environmental Friction):**
- Natural circumstances (weather, terrain, darkness)
- Impersonal systems (gravity, time, distance)
- Resource limitations (money, energy, materials)
- Circumstantial obstacles (broken equipment, traffic)
- **Key:** Not intentionally imposed by another agent to control this specific behavior

**C3 (Heteronomous Action):**
- Authority commands (boss, police, parent)
- Social pressure with control intent (ostracism threats, group coercion)
- Coercive threats (punishment, harm, sanctions)
- Institutional demands (when experienced as external control)
- **Key:** Another agent deliberately constraining the will

**Philosophical support:**
- Berlin: Only restrictions imposed by other people affect political freedom
- Coercion theory: Coercion requires intentional imposition by an agent
- SDT: Controlled motivation arises from external demands by others

**Ambiguous cases:**
- Internalized social norms (depends on whether agent identifies with them)
- Institutional structures (depends on whether experienced as external control)
- Cultural expectations (depends on degree of internalization)

---

### Criterion 2: Mechanism of Influence

**Question:** Does this constraint operate on first-order desires (execution) or second-order volitions (will-formation)?

**C0 (Environmental Friction):**
- Affects **first-order execution only**
- Agent knows what they want to want (second-order volition intact)
- Obstacle makes action harder but doesn't change what agent values
- **Example:** Locked door - agent still wants to leave, just can't execute

**C3 (Heteronomous Action):**
- Affects **second-order volitions**
- Creates first-order desires that conflict with what agent wants to want
- Shapes the agent's will, not just execution difficulty
- **Example:** Boss threat - creates desire to comply that agent doesn't endorse

**Philosophical support:**
- Frankfurt: Free will requires alignment between first-order desires and second-order volitions
- Coercion creates first-order desires (fear-based compliance) that override second-order volitions
- Willing vs unwilling addict: same action, different relationship to second-order volitions

**Test questions:**
1. "What do you want to do?" (first-order desire)
2. "What do you want to want to do?" (second-order volition)
3. Are these aligned or in conflict?
   - Aligned → C0
   - Conflict → C3

---

### Criterion 3: Reasons-Responsiveness

**Question:** Can the agent still recognize and respond to reasons, or is their rational deliberation bypassed/overridden?

**C0 (Environmental Friction):**
- Agent remains **reasons-responsive**
- Can recognize reasons for alternative actions
- Would respond differently if reasons changed
- Obstacle only prevents execution, not deliberation
- **Example:** No money for taxi - agent recognizes walking is alternative, deliberates about costs/benefits

**C3 (Heteronomous Action):**
- Agent's **reasons-responsiveness is compromised**
- External pressure bypasses rational deliberation
- Compliance mechanism overrides reason-tracking
- Would not respond differently even if reasons changed (because external pressure dominates)
- **Example:** Boss demands immediate compliance - agent complies without deliberating about whether it's justified

**Philosophical support:**
- Fischer & Ravizza: Moral responsibility requires reasons-responsiveness
- Guidance control requires the mechanism be appropriately responsive to reasons
- Compulsion and coercion compromise reasons-responsiveness

**Test question:** "If the reasons for/against this action changed, would the agent respond differently?"
- YES (tracking reasons) → C0
- NO (external pressure dominates) → C3

---

### Criterion 4: Autonomy Experience (SDT)

**Question:** Does the agent experience their motivation as autonomous (willingly endorsing) or controlled (feeling compelled)?

**C0 (Environmental Friction):**
- Agent **willingly endorses** their action
- Feels they are choosing, even if options are limited
- Experiences autonomy need as satisfied
- Internal locus of causality
- **Phenomenology:** "This is difficult, but it's my choice"

**C3 (Heteronomous Action):**
- Agent feels **compelled** or controlled
- Does not willingly endorse their action
- Experiences autonomy need as thwarted
- External locus of causality
- **Phenomenology:** "I have to do this" or "I have no choice"

**Philosophical support:**
- SDT: Autonomous motivation = willingly endorsing behavior
- Controlled motivation = feeling compelled by external pressure
- Same behavior can be autonomous or controlled depending on experience

**Important nuance:**
- Controlled motivation includes both:
  - External regulation (reward/punishment)
  - Introjected regulation (guilt, shame, "should")
- Even if no external agent is currently present, introjected regulation may indicate C3 origin

**Test questions:**
1. "Do you feel you're choosing this, or do you feel you have to?"
2. "If this external pressure disappeared, would you still do this?"
   - YES → Autonomous (C0)
   - NO → Controlled (C3)

---

### Criterion 5: Will-Overbearing

**Question:** Does this constraint merely make options less attractive, or does it actually override the agent's will?

**C0 (Environmental Friction):**
- Makes options **less attractive** but doesn't override will
- Agent's preferences remain intact
- All options still genuinely available (even if difficult)
- Agent chooses based on their own cost-benefit analysis
- **Example:** Expensive taxi - agent considers whether value justifies cost

**C3 (Heteronomous Action):**
- **Overrides the will** - makes one option "irresistible"
- Creates options that "no reasonable person could refuse"
- Agent's own preferences become irrelevant
- Choice is technically available but not genuinely available
- **Example:** "Do this or you're fired" - creates first-order desire that overrides agent's preferences

**Philosophical support:**
- Frankfurt: Coercion requires arousing desire that overbears the victim's will
- Not just making options less attractive, but actually overriding deliberation
- Difference between difficulty and compulsion

**Spectrum:**
- **Mild influence:** Makes option slightly less attractive → C0
- **Moderate pressure:** Makes option significantly less attractive → C0/C3 boundary
- **Strong coercion:** Makes option so unattractive that no reasonable person could choose it → C3

**Test question:** "Could a reasonable person in this situation genuinely choose otherwise?"
- YES (even if difficult) → C0
- NO (option effectively eliminated) → C3

## Integrated Classification Algorithm

### Step 1: Source Check (Criterion 1)

**Is this constraint imposed by another agent with intent to control behavior?**

- NO → **Strong presumption of C0** (proceed to step 2 for confirmation)
- YES → **Candidate for C3** (proceed through all remaining criteria)

### Step 2: Mechanism Check (Criterion 2)

**Does this affect first-order execution or second-order volitions?**

- First-order only (execution difficulty) → **Points toward C0**
- Second-order (shapes what agent wants to want) → **Points toward C3**

**Test:** Ask the agent:
- "What do you want to do?" (first-order)
- "What do you want to want to do?" (second-order)
- **If aligned → C0; if conflict → C3**

### Step 3: Reasons-Responsiveness Check (Criterion 3)

**Can the agent recognize and respond to reasons?**

- YES (still deliberating, would respond to changed reasons) → **Points toward C0**
- NO (external pressure bypasses deliberation) → **Points toward C3**

**Test:** "If the reasons for this changed, would you respond differently?"
- YES → C0
- NO → C3

### Step 4: Autonomy Experience Check (Criterion 4)

**Does the agent willingly endorse their action or feel compelled?**

- Willingly endorses → **Points toward C0**
- Feels compelled → **Points toward C3**

**Test:** "Do you feel you're choosing this, or do you feel you have to?"
- Choosing → C0
- Have to → C3

### Step 5: Will-Overbearing Check (Criterion 5)

**Does this merely make options less attractive or override the will?**

- Makes options less attractive → **Points toward C0**
- Overrides the will → **Points toward C3**

**Test:** "Could a reasonable person genuinely choose otherwise?"
- YES → C0
- NO → C3

### Decision Rule

**Classify as C0 if:**
- Passes all 5 criteria for C0, OR
- Passes 4/5 with one ambiguous

**Classify as C3 if:**
- Passes 4-5 criteria for C3

**Classify as AMBIGUOUS if:**
- Mixed results (2-3 criteria each direction)
- Requires deeper analysis or context-dependent assessment

## Application Examples

### Example 1: Locked Door

**Scenario:** Agent wants to leave house, finds door locked

**Analysis:**
1. **Source:** Not imposed by another agent (circumstantial) → C0
2. **Mechanism:** First-order only (still wants to leave) → C0
3. **Reasons-responsive:** Yes (would try window if door impossible) → C0
4. **Autonomy:** Willingly endorses desire to leave → C0
5. **Will-overbearing:** No (just makes exit difficult) → C0

**Result: Clear C0 (Environmental Friction)**

---

### Example 2: Boss Command

**Scenario:** Boss demands employee work weekend or face firing

**Analysis:**
1. **Source:** Another agent with control intent → C3
2. **Mechanism:** Creates first-order desire (work weekend) conflicting with second-order volition (wants to value work-life balance) → C3
3. **Reasons-responsive:** Compromised (compliance overrides deliberation) → C3
4. **Autonomy:** Feels compelled, does not willingly endorse → C3
5. **Will-overbearing:** Yes (reasonable person couldn't refuse given job dependency) → C3

**Result: Clear C3 (Heteronomous Action)**

---

### Example 3: Internalized Professional Norm

**Scenario:** Agent "knows they should dress professionally" for work

**Analysis:**
1. **Source:** Originally social (dress code), but may be internalized → AMBIGUOUS
2. **Mechanism:** Could be first-order (just habit) OR second-order (feels "should") → DEPENDS
3. **Reasons-responsive:** Probably yes (could choose not to if reasons changed) → C0
4. **Autonomy:** **KEY QUESTION:** Does agent willingly endorse or feel compelled?
   - If willingly endorses ("I value professionalism") → C0
   - If feels compelled ("I have to or they'll judge me") → C3
5. **Will-overbearing:** Probably not (could choose otherwise) → C0

**Result: DEPENDS on autonomy experience**
- If internalized and endorsed → C0
- If still felt as external pressure → C3

**Test question:** "If you could redesign workplace norms, would you keep this requirement?"
- YES → C0 (internalized)
- NO → C3 (external pressure)

---

### Example 4: Social Anxiety (Friend's Gathering)

**Scenario:** Agent feels anxious about speaking up at friend's gathering

**Analysis:**
1. **Source:** Not imposed by another agent (internal state) → C0
2. **Mechanism:** First-order (makes speaking difficult) → C0
3. **Reasons-responsive:** Yes (would speak if stakes changed) → C0
4. **Autonomy:** Willingly endorses desire to speak (just anxious about execution) → C0
5. **Will-overbearing:** No (anxiety makes it harder, not impossible) → C0

**Result: Clear C0 (Environmental Friction - psychological)**

**Note:** This assumes the anxiety is not the result of current social coercion. If friends were actively silencing the agent, that would be C3.

---

### Example 5: Social Ostracism Threat

**Scenario:** Friend group threatens to exclude agent if they don't comply with group decision

**Analysis:**
1. **Source:** Other agents with control intent → C3
2. **Mechanism:** Creates first-order desire (comply) conflicting with second-order volition (values independent thought) → C3
3. **Reasons-responsive:** Compromised (fear of exclusion bypasses deliberation) → C3
4. **Autonomy:** Feels compelled, does not willingly endorse compliance → C3
5. **Will-overbearing:** Probably yes (ostracism is severe social punishment) → C3

**Result: Clear C3 (Heteronomous Action - social coercion)**

---

### Example 6: Poverty / Resource Constraint

**Scenario:** Agent can't afford food and must choose between rent and groceries

**Analysis:**
1. **Source:** Not imposed by specific agent (systemic/circumstantial) → C0 (but see note below)
2. **Mechanism:** First-order (limits options) not second-order (still values both rent and food) → C0
3. **Reasons-responsive:** Yes (would respond if resources changed) → C0
4. **Autonomy:** Complex - may willingly endorse choices given constraints → C0
5. **Will-overbearing:** No (difficult choice, but still genuine deliberation) → C0

**Result: Probably C0 (Environmental Friction - resource constraint)**

**Important caveat:** This analysis treats poverty as circumstantial. Critical theorists would argue that systemic poverty IS agent-imposed (by structural oppression) and should be C3. This highlights a limitation of the individual-focused framework.

---

### Example 7: Parental Command (Young Child)

**Scenario:** Parent tells young child "Go to bed now"

**Analysis:**
1. **Source:** Another agent with control intent → C3
2. **Mechanism:** May affect both first and second-order (child hasn't developed stable second-order volitions) → C3
3. **Reasons-responsive:** Limited (child may not have capacity for full deliberation) → C3
4. **Autonomy:** Feels compelled (does not willingly endorse) → C3
5. **Will-overbearing:** Yes (child cannot reasonably refuse parental authority) → C3

**Result: Clear C3 (Heteronomous Action)**

**Note:** This is appropriate for young children who haven't developed autonomy. For adult children, the analysis might differ.

---

### Example 8: Trauma Response (Freeze)

**Scenario:** Agent freezes when confronted with trauma trigger

**Analysis:**
1. **Source:** Not imposed by another agent in the moment → C0 (but see note)
2. **Mechanism:** Second-order affected (agent doesn't want to want to freeze) → C3
3. **Reasons-responsive:** NO (freeze bypasses deliberation) → C3
4. **Autonomy:** Not willingly endorsed → C3
5. **Will-overbearing:** YES (freeze overrides will) → C3

**Result: MIXED (3/5 point to C3, but source is not another agent)**

**Implication:** This suggests trauma responses may need a SEPARATE CATEGORY. The original trauma was C3, but the current response is neither pure C0 nor C3.

**Recommendation:** Consider whether VCM needs a **C3-derived** category for responses that originated in heteronomous action (trauma) but are triggered by current circumstances.

## Boundary Case Decision Tree

### When Criteria Conflict

**If Source = Natural/Circumstantial BUT Mechanism/Reasons-Responsiveness/Autonomy point to C3:**
- Likely indicates **internalized C3** or **C3-derived state**
- Examples: Internalized oppression, trauma responses
- **Classification:** Consider separate category or context-dependent assessment

**If Source = Agent-Imposed BUT all other criteria point to C0:**
- Likely indicates **fully internalized norm** that is now autonomous
- Examples: Internalized professional values, adopted cultural practices
- **Classification:** C0 (environmental friction from internalized structure)

**If Autonomy Experience conflicts with objective assessment:**
- Agent says "willingly choosing" but reasons-responsiveness compromised
- **Risk:** Rationalization or false consciousness
- **Recommendation:** Weight objective criteria (reasons-responsiveness, mechanism) over self-report

**If Will-Overbearing is borderline:**
- Moderate pressure that makes option very unattractive but not impossible
- **Classification:** Depends on other criteria
  - If 3-4 other criteria point to C3 → Classify as C3
  - If 3-4 other criteria point to C0 → Classify as C0

## Special Cases and Extensions

### Internalized Constraints

**Philosophical insight:** Constraints can MIGRATE from C3 to C0 through internalization.

**Example:**
- **Initial state:** "I must exercise because doctor orders it" → C3 (controlled motivation)
- **Internalized state:** "I exercise because I value health" → C0 (autonomous motivation)

**Classification rule:** Assess the CURRENT relationship, not the origin.

**Test:** "If the external source disappeared (doctor never mentioned it), would you still do this?"
- YES → Internalized, now C0
- NO → Still C3

---

### Structural and Systemic Constraints

**Philosophical debate:** Are institutional structures and systemic inequalities C0 or C3?

**Individualist view:** If no specific agent is controlling you in this moment → C0
**Structural view:** Systems ARE agents (collective agency) → C3

**Practical approach for VCM:**
- **Immediate institutional demands** (boss order, police command) → C3
- **Background structural constraints** (poverty, discrimination) → C0 for individual analysis
- **Note:** Acknowledge the philosophical debate; VCM focuses on individual agency

---

### Habit and Automaticity

**Question:** Are automatic habits C0 or C3?

**Analysis depends on:**
1. **Origin:** Was habit formed autonomously or through external control?
   - Self-initiated → C0
   - Externally imposed → Consider C3 origin

2. **Current endorsement:** Does agent identify with habit?
   - YES → C0 (even if origin was C3)
   - NO → May still be C3 (unwanted habit)

**Example:**
- **Autonomous habit:** Morning exercise routine agent values → C0
- **Unwanted habit:** Nail-biting agent wants to stop → C0 (physical friction)
- **Socially imposed habit:** Flinching at criticism from abusive relationship → C3-derived

---

### Cultural Context and Collectivism

**Philosophical concern:** Is the C0/C3 distinction culturally biased toward Western individualism?

**Consideration:** In collectivist cultures, social harmony may be:
- **Western view:** External pressure (C3)
- **Collectivist view:** Core value, fully internalized (C0)

**VCM approach:** Assess the individual's relationship to the cultural norm:
- If agent identifies with collectivist values → C0
- If agent feels pressured to conform against their values → C3

**Key:** Don't assume cultural norms are automatically C3 just because they're social. Assess internalization and endorsement.

## Summary: Quick Reference Guide

### C0 (Environmental Friction) - Quick Checklist

✓ Natural, circumstantial, or impersonal source
✓ Affects execution difficulty, not will-formation
✓ Agent remains reasons-responsive
✓ Agent willingly endorses action despite difficulty
✓ Makes options less attractive but doesn't override will

**Prototype:** Locked door, lack of money, cold weather

---

### C3 (Heteronomous Action) - Quick Checklist

✓ Another agent imposing control
✓ Affects will-formation (second-order volitions)
✓ Compromises reasons-responsiveness
✓ Agent feels compelled, not willing
✓ Overrides the will

**Prototype:** Boss threat, coercive command, ostracism threat

---

### When in Doubt

**Primary question:** "Does this compromise the agent's reasons-responsiveness and second-order volitions?"
- NO → C0
- YES → C3

**Secondary question:** "Does the agent willingly endorse their action?"
- YES → C0
- NO → C3

## Philosophical Confidence Levels

**High confidence distinctions:**
- Physical obstacles vs authority commands → C0 vs C3 (all frameworks agree)
- Resource constraints vs coercive threats → C0 vs C3 (all frameworks agree)
- Natural circumstances vs intentional control → C0 vs C3 (all frameworks agree)

**Medium confidence distinctions:**
- Mild social influence vs strong social pressure → Depends on degree
- Internalized norms vs external pressure → Depends on autonomy experience
- Institutional structures → Context-dependent

**Low confidence / Ambiguous:**
- Trauma responses (neither pure C0 nor pure C3)
- Internalized oppression (appears C0 but may mask C3)
- Systemic constraints (philosophical debate about agency)
