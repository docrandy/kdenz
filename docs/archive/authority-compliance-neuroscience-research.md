# Authority Compliance and Agentic States: Neuroscience Research Summary

**Research Date:** 2026-02-10
**Focus:** Neural mechanisms of authority compliance, agentic states, and implications for volitional classification

---

## Executive Summary

Recent neuroscience research (2015-2025) provides strong evidence that **authority compliance fundamentally alters neural processing** compared to voluntary action. Key findings:

1. **Coercion reduces sense of agency** through temporal binding effects and reduced neural processing of action outcomes
2. **Obedience reduces cognitive conflict** - following orders bypasses effortful deliberation (reduced mid-frontal theta activity)
3. **Brain regions show differential activation** - dmPFC acts as driver of diffusion of responsibility; mPFC activity relates to preserved agency under coercion
4. **Processing mode appears hybrid** - not purely automatic, but substantially reduces deliberative load

**Critical Distinction:** Authority compliance does NOT fully bypass volitional systems but significantly reduces the cognitive effort and sense of agency normally associated with voluntary action.

## 1. Milgram's Agentic State Theory - Modern Neuroscience Evidence

### Core Theory
Milgram's "agentic state" describes a psychological state where individuals see themselves as agents carrying out another person's wishes, leading to reduced personal responsibility and agency.

### Modern Empirical Support

**Caspar et al. (2016, 2024) - Landmark Studies:**
- Used brain imaging to demonstrate objective evidence for different neural processing under coercion vs. voluntary action
- Found that **coercion increases the perceived time between action and outcomes** (temporal binding effect)
- Showed that **coercion reduces neural processing of action outcomes** (reduced N1 ERP amplitude)
- Replicated findings in multiple contexts (financial harm, physical harm to others)

**Key Quote:** "People who obey orders may subjectively experience their actions as closer to passive movements than fully voluntary actions."

### Behavioral Evidence
Recent replication extended Caspar's 2016 findings in online settings where participants served only as agents (similar to original Milgram studies), controlling for order of free vs. forced choice blocks.

**Sources:**
- [Obedience induces agentic shifts by increasing the perceived time between own action and results](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11263708/)
- [Coercion Changes the Sense of Agency in the Human Brain](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4791480/)
- [Coercion Changes the Sense of Agency in the Human Brain - PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26898470/)

## 2. Neural Mechanisms of Authority Compliance

### 2.1 Reduced Sense of Agency

**Temporal Binding Effects:**
- Coercion increased perceived interval between actions and their consequences
- Normal voluntary action shows "temporal compression" - actions and outcomes feel closer together
- Under coercion, this compression is reduced or absent
- Suggests weakened action-outcome linkage in the brain

**Neural Processing Changes:**
- **Reduced N1 amplitude** under coercion vs. free choice
- N1 is an early event-related potential reflecting sensory processing
- Coercion produces "anticipatory reduction of sensory processing for action outcomes"
- Involves both downregulation of perceptual gains and temporal distancing

**Interpretation:** The brain processes commanded actions differently from voluntary actions at a fundamental neural level, reducing the normal integration of action and outcome.

**Sources:**
- [Coercion Changes the Sense of Agency in the Human Brain](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4791480/)
- [Neuro Milgram – Your brain takes less ownership of actions](https://www.bps.org.uk/research-digest/neuro-milgram-your-brain-takes-less-ownership-actions-you-perform-under-coercion)

### 2.2 Cognitive Conflict Reduction

**Mid-Frontal Theta Activity (2024 Study):**
- **Mid-frontal theta activity was reduced** when participants obeyed experimenter's orders vs. acting voluntarily
- This occurred even though action outcomes were similar
- Mid-frontal theta is associated with cognitive conflict, effort, and deliberation
- "Obeying orders diminishes cognitive conflict preceding moral decisions that could harm another person"

**Participant Reports:**
- Explicit reports indicate it is "easier" to just follow orders
- Following orders reduces cognitive load associated with decision-making
- Suggests obedience reduces effortful processing

**Sources:**
- [Conflict experience and resolution underlying obedience to authority](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10333324/)
- [Obedience to authority reduces cognitive conflict before an action](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38975965/)

### 2.3 Brain Regions Involved

#### Medial Prefrontal Cortex (mPFC)
**Role in Authority Context:**
- Increased activity when **preserving agency under coercion**
- Greater mPFC activity associated with:
  - Preserved temporal binding under coercion
  - Maintained sense of responsibility for action outcomes
- May reflect process of overcoming conflict between obeying authority and preference for autonomous action

**Role in Social Hierarchy:**
- Ventral mPFC shows increased activity when aligning with higher-ranking partners
- Rostral mPFC increases when conforming to superior partner, decreases when conforming to inferior partner
- Central to processing social hierarchies and conformity

**Core Functions:**
- Integrates information from multiple cortical and subcortical areas
- Involved in: cognitive processing, emotion regulation, motivation, sociability
- Learns associations between context, events, and adaptive responses
- Performance monitoring, error detection, conflict resolution

**Sources:**
- [The obedient mind and the volitional brain](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8553174/)
- [Distinctive Roles of Medial Prefrontal Cortex Subregions in Strategic Conformity to Social Hierarchy](https://www.jneurosci.org/content/43/36/6330)
- [Medial prefrontal cortex in neurological diseases](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6766703/)

#### Dorsomedial Prefrontal Cortex (dmPFC)
**Role in Diffusion of Responsibility:**
- Acts as **driver of the punishment network** during diffusion of responsibility
- Modulates other regions: anterior insula, vmPFC, precuneus
- Adjusts altruistic punishment behavior based on presence of others

**Core Functions:**
- Processes sense of self and self-referential outcomes
- Theory of mind, empathy, moral judgments
- Predicts outcomes in social, strategic contexts
- Central to self-awareness and maintaining continuous sense of identity
- Activity level predicts altruistic behavior

**Role in Decision-Making:**
- Reflects **deliberation time** (distinct from confidence in choices)
- More dorsal regions concerned with metacognitive variables rather than option valuation
- Dissociation between effort attached to options vs. effort invested in deliberation

**Sources:**
- [Diffusion of responsibility attenuates altruistic punishment - fMRI study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26608776/)
- [Beyond self-serving bias: diffusion of responsibility reduces sense of agency](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5390744/)
- [Dorsomedial prefrontal cortex - Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dorsomedial_prefrontal_cortex)
- [Value, Confidence, Deliberation: A Functional Partition of the Medial Prefrontal Cortex](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9295841/)

#### Temporoparietal Junction (TPJ)
**Role in Agency and Self-Other Distinction:**
- Increased bilateral TPJ activity during social trials (following orders) vs. nonsocial trials
- Critical for **self-other distinction** and sense of agency
- Right TPJ specifically involved in: own-body imagery, agency, embodied perspective-taking
- Positioned at "neural interface between mind and matter"

**Core Functions:**
- Integrates information from thalamus, limbic system, visual, auditory, somatosensory systems
- Processes both external environment and internal body information
- Represents mental states and distinguishes between self and other mental states
- Left TPJ (with frontal cortex) + right TPJ both involved in theory of mind

**Clinical Relevance:**
- Damage produces out-of-body experiences
- Dysfunction linked to Tourette syndrome, schizophrenia, autism spectrum disorder, OCD
- Problems with self-other distinctions may reflect vulnerability to these conditions

**Sources:**
- [Losing Control in Social Situations: Neural Processes Related to Sense of Agency](https://www.eneuro.org/content/5/1/ENEURO.0336-17.2018)
- [The Role of the Temporoparietal Junction in Self-Other Distinction](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31676934/)
- [Temporoparietal junction - Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temporoparietal_junction)

#### Anterior Insula
**Role in Norm Violation Processing:**
- Shows **attenuated responses** to norm violations when others are present (diffusion of responsibility)
- Normally signals norm violations and their salience
- Reduced activity suggests diminished moral processing under distributed responsibility

**Sources:**
- [Diffusion of responsibility attenuates altruistic punishment](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26608776/)

## 3. Diffusion of Responsibility - Neural and Psychological Mechanisms

### Neural Network Changes

**When Others Are Present:**
1. **Reduced anterior insula activity** - diminished norm violation signaling
2. **Increased vmPFC and precuneus activity** - enhanced value calculation of norm violations
3. **Increased dmPFC activity** - enhanced thinking about others
4. **dmPFC acts as network driver** - modulates other regions to adjust punishment behavior

### Outcome Monitoring

**EEG Studies:**
- Presence of another potential agent **reduces sense of agency over outcomes**
- **Reduced feedback-related negativity amplitude** - less neural response to outcomes
- Suggests weakened neural linkage between actions and their outcomes
- "The presence of other agents may lead to diffusion of responsibility by weakening the neural linkage between one's actions and their outcomes"

**Sources:**
- [Diffusion of responsibility attenuates altruistic punishment - fMRI](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26608776/)
- [Beyond self-serving bias: diffusion of responsibility reduces sense of agency](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5390744/)

## 4. Automatic vs. Deliberative Processing: Key Evidence

### Evidence for REDUCED Deliberation Under Authority

**1. Reduced Mid-Frontal Theta Activity**
- Mid-frontal theta = marker of cognitive effort, conflict, deliberation
- Significantly reduced when obeying orders vs. voluntary action
- Indicates less effortful processing under obedience

**2. Participant Self-Reports**
- Participants explicitly report it's "easier" to follow orders
- Reduced subjective sense of effort
- Aligns with neural evidence of reduced deliberation

**3. Reduced Cognitive Conflict**
- Moral decisions normally involve conflict processing
- This conflict is diminished under obedience
- Suggests bypass of normal evaluative processes

### Evidence for PRESERVED Deliberation (Some Contexts)

**1. Medial Frontal Gyrus Activity**
- Increased mFG activity when preserving agency under coercion
- May reflect "overcoming conflict" between obedience and autonomous preference
- Suggests some individuals engage deliberative systems even under coercion

**2. Not Pure Automaticity**
- People still consciously process the commands
- Can choose to disobey (though rarely do)
- Engagement of social cognition networks (TPJ, dmPFC)

### Dual-System Framework

Research supports dual-system model:

**Type 1 (Automatic):**
- Fast, efficient, minimal attention
- Stimulus-driven, goal-independent
- Hard to modify, occurs inevitably when triggered
- Does NOT require working memory

**Type 2 (Deliberative):**
- Slower, effortful, resource-intensive
- Goal-dependent, introspectively accessible
- Requires controlled attention and working memory

**Authority Compliance Appears to be:**
- **Primarily Type 1 with reduced Type 2 engagement**
- Commands trigger response tendency that is easier to follow than resist
- Some deliberative processing still occurs (can be overridden) but is substantially reduced
- Not pure automaticity (like reflexes) but significantly less effortful than autonomous decision-making

**Sources:**
- [Conflict experience and resolution underlying obedience to authority](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10333324/)
- [The evolution and devolution of cognitive control](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4468419/)
- [Controlled & automatic processing: behavior, theory, and biological mechanisms](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/pdf/10.1207/s15516709cog2703_8)

## 5. Virtual Milgram fMRI Studies

### Methodology
- Partial replication of Milgram paradigm in immersive virtual environment
- Participants administered "pain" to virtual human
- Event-related fMRI during observation of victim in pain vs. not in pain

### Key Findings

**Limited Empathic Response:**
- Activation in areas involved in affective processing
- **NO activation** in areas commonly associated with affect sharing (ACC, insula)
- Virtual learner receiving shocks did not activate typical empathic response areas
- Suggests obedience context may suppress empathy networks

**Social Cognition Activation:**
- Increased bilateral TPJ, precuneus, middle frontal gyrus during social trials
- Consistent with self-other distinction and agency processing

**Prosocial Disobedience Research:**
- Recent studies examine neurocognitive processes in prosocial disobedience
- Key processes: sense of agency (SoA) and empathy for pain
- Obeying orders to inflict harm impacts different neurocognitive processes
- Notably: moral conflict (mid-frontal theta EEG activity)

**Sources:**
- [Virtual Milgram: Empathic Concern or Personal Distress? - fMRI](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2769551/)
- [Virtual milgram: empathic concern or personal distress?](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/neuro.09.029.2009/full)
- [fMRI investigation of prosocial disobedience](https://osf.io/xcgak/)

## 6. Key Distinction: Volitional System Engaged or Bypassed?

### Evidence for "Engaged but Altered" (Not Bypassed)

**Volitional Systems ARE Engaged:**
1. Prefrontal cortex activity is present (mPFC, dmPFC, mFG)
2. Social cognition networks activated (TPJ, dmPFC for theory of mind)
3. Can be overridden (people can disobey, though rarely)
4. Some preserved sense of agency and responsibility in some individuals

**BUT Processing Is Fundamentally Different:**
1. Reduced cognitive conflict (mid-frontal theta)
2. Reduced sense of agency (temporal binding effects)
3. Reduced outcome monitoring (N1 amplitude, feedback-related negativity)
4. Subjectively experienced as "easier" - less effortful
5. Actions feel "closer to passive movements than fully voluntary actions"

### Answer to Key Question

**"When someone follows a boss's command, is the volitional system engaged or bypassed?"**

**Answer:** The volitional system is **engaged but substantially altered**. It is not bypassed completely (as in reflexes or trauma freeze), but:

1. Deliberative processes are **reduced** (less cognitive conflict, effort)
2. Sense of agency is **diminished** (temporal binding effects)
3. Action-outcome integration is **weakened** (reduced neural processing)
4. The default response tendency is to **comply** (requires more effort to resist than to obey)

### Implications for VCM Classification

**C0 (Pre-Volitional) vs. C3 (Volitional Constraint)?**

Authority compliance does NOT fit cleanly into either category:

**Arguments for C0 (Pre-Volitional):**
- Reduced deliberation (mid-frontal theta)
- Diminished sense of agency
- Subjectively feels "easier" - less volitional effort
- Weakened action-outcome integration
- Can trigger without conscious evaluation

**Arguments for C3 (Volitional Constraint):**
- Prefrontal systems still engaged
- Can be overridden with effort
- Some preserved agency and responsibility
- Not reflexive or automatic like trauma freeze
- Social cognition networks actively process the situation

**Proposed Resolution:**
Authority compliance represents a **hybrid state** - it is **volitional system engagement under altered conditions**.

Recommend classification:
- **Simple compliance with clear authority** → C3 (volitional but constrained by social hierarchy)
- **Coerced compliance with threat** → C0/C1 boundary (approaching pre-volitional due to survival/safety concerns)
- **Hierarchical deference in organizations** → C3 (volitional constraint by social structure)

The distinction depends on:
1. Degree of coercion vs. voluntary respect for hierarchy
2. Presence of threat or punishment
3. Individual's subjective experience of agency
4. Context (military, workplace, research study)

## 8. Key Takeaways for VCM Framework

### What Happens Psychologically During Authority Compliance?

1. **Sense of agency is reduced** but not eliminated
2. **Cognitive conflict/deliberation is reduced** - easier to obey than resist
3. **Action-outcome integration is weakened** - brain processes consequences differently
4. **Responsibility feels diffused** - especially when others are present
5. **Social cognition networks engage** - processing authority relationship, not pure automaticity

### Brain Mechanisms That Distinguish Authority Compliance from Autonomous Choice

1. **Temporal binding effects** - increased perceived time between action and outcome
2. **Reduced N1 amplitude** - diminished sensory processing of outcomes
3. **Reduced mid-frontal theta** - less cognitive conflict and deliberation
4. **Altered prefrontal network activity** - different pattern than voluntary choice
5. **Enhanced social cognition** - TPJ, dmPFC processing social context

### Evidence for Automatic vs. Deliberative Processing

**Not Purely Automatic Because:**
- Prefrontal systems still engaged
- Can be overridden (though difficult)
- Social cognition networks active
- Some preserved sense of agency/responsibility

**Not Fully Deliberative Because:**
- Reduced cognitive conflict
- Reduced effortful processing
- Diminished sense of agency
- Subjectively "easier" than autonomous choice
- Weakened action-outcome integration

**Conclusion:** Authority compliance is a **hybrid processing mode** that sits between pure automaticity and full deliberative control. It engages volitional systems but in an altered state that reduces effort, agency, and outcome monitoring.

### Recommended VCM Classification Approach

**Context-Dependent Classification:**

1. **Pure hierarchical deference** (e.g., employee following boss's reasonable request)
   - **Classification: C3 (Volitional - Social Hierarchy)**
   - Rationale: Volitional systems engaged, can be overridden, social constraint

2. **Coerced compliance with threat** (e.g., "do this or you're fired")
   - **Classification: C0/C1 boundary (Pre-Volitional/Survival)**
   - Rationale: Survival concerns, reduced agency, approaching trauma-like processing

3. **Organizational compliance** (e.g., following company policy)
   - **Classification: C3 (Volitional - Social Hierarchy)**
   - Rationale: Deliberate choice to maintain employment, clear alternatives

4. **Military/emergency orders** (e.g., combat situations, first responders)
   - **Classification: C1 or C2 (Survival/Instinct or Threat)**
   - Rationale: Time pressure, survival context, trained automatic responses

The key distinction is whether the compliance involves:
- **Coercion + threat** → C0/C1 (pre-volitional or survival)
- **Social hierarchy respect** → C3 (volitional constraint)
- **Trained automatic response** → C2 (instinct/trained response)

## 9. Limitations and Future Research Directions

### Study Limitations

1. **Lab vs. Real World:** Most studies use virtual paradigms or mild financial harms
2. **Individual Differences:** Wide variation in how people respond to authority
3. **Cultural Context:** Most research conducted in Western contexts
4. **Time Course:** Little research on how compliance changes over time
5. **Real Consequences:** Virtual victims don't provide same neural response as real harm

### Future Research Needed

1. **Longitudinal studies** of how chronic authority compliance affects neural processing
2. **Cross-cultural neuroscience** of authority responses
3. **Individual difference studies** - what makes some people more/less susceptible?
4. **Real-world fMRI** studies in organizational contexts (difficult but valuable)
5. **Intervention studies** - can training alter neural response to authority?
6. **Developmental studies** - how does authority compliance develop across lifespan?
