## Executive Summary

This document defines the refined classification framework for distinguishing pre-volitional constraints (C0) from volitional constraints (C3+) in the Volitional Chain Model. Based on convergent evidence from neuroscience, behavioral economics, and philosophy, the framework uses a two-stage test that correctly classifies environmental friction as C3 while maintaining C0 for true input constraints.

**Key Change:** Environmental Friction (formerly C0-1) reclassified to C3-8 based on agency preservation and deliberative processing evidence.

---

## Part 1: The Two-Stage Classification System

### Stage 1: Volitional Entry Test (VET)

**Question:** Does information about the goal and action path enter the volitional processing system (prefrontal cortex, ACC, basal ganglia)?

- **NO** → **C0** (Pre-Volitional Input Constraint)
- **YES** → Continue to Stage 2

### Stage 2: Agency Preservation Test (APT)

**Question:** Does the constraint preserve or suppress agency circuits during processing?

#### Agency PRESERVED → C3 (Volitional Gate)

**Neural Markers:**
- ✅ Pre-SMA activity (intention formation)
- ✅ Normal temporal binding (action attributed to self)
- ✅ High ACC conflict signals (effortful deliberation)
- ✅ Integrated action-outcome processing
- ✅ Metacognitive awareness (confidence encoding)

**Phenomenology:**
- "I choose not to"
- "Too much effort"
- "Not worth it"
- Deliberate cost-benefit weighing

**Example:** "It's raining, so I decide not to go for a walk."

#### Agency SUPPRESSED → C0 or C3-9

**Neural Markers:**
- ❌ Reduced mPFC activity (ownership suppressed)
- ❌ Altered temporal binding (action feels passive)
- ❌ Reduced cognitive conflict (easier to comply)
- ❌ Weakened action-outcome integration
- ❌ "Agentic state" activation

**Phenomenology:**
- "I must"
- "I can't"
- "No choice"
- "They made me"

**Example:** "My boss ordered me to attend, so I had to go."

## Part 2: C0 Subcategories

### C0a: Healthy Pre-Volitional

**Definition:** Information literally cannot enter volitional circuits.

**Examples:**
- Sensory limitations (ultraviolet light not perceived)
- Physical disability (paralysis - execution blocked)
- Neural damage (signal cannot reach cortex)
- Structural impossibility (locked door with no key)

**Test:** Even with infinite motivation, action remains impossible due to physical/biological constraints.

**Intervention:** Environmental redesign, medical treatment, assistive technology.

### C0b: Trauma-Freeze

**Definition:** Subcortical override that bypasses cortical evaluation.

**Mechanism:** Amygdala→PAG pathway (<150ms) triggers autonomic freeze before prefrontal cortex can engage.

**Examples:**
- Trauma-triggered immobility
- Threat-induced freeze response
- Panic-related paralysis

**Test:** Initial trigger operates pre-volitionally; sustained freeze may involve cortical modulation but onset is subcortical.

**Intervention:** Trauma therapy, somatic experiencing, polyvagal-informed approaches.

### C0c: Coercive Authority

**Definition:** Severe coercion with explicit threat where agency is so severely suppressed it approaches trauma-like processing.

**Examples:**
- "Do this or you're fired" (survival threat)
- Military combat orders under threat
- Abusive relationship commands

**Test:** Fear/survival concerns + extreme agency suppression; distinguishable from mild social pressure which preserves agency.

**Intervention:** Safety planning, exit strategies, legal support, therapeutic intervention for autonomy restoration.

## Part 3: C3 Categories

### C3-1 through C3-7: Existing Categories

(Unchanged - see VCM Root Cause Database)

### C3-8: Environmental Friction (NEW - Moved from C0-1)

**Definition:** Environmental factors (rain, distance, mess) that increase perceived effort cost through deliberative cost-benefit analysis with full agency preserved.

**Mechanism:**
- Information enters prefrontal cortex ✅
- ACC encodes effort costs ✅
- Basal ganglia compute cost-benefit tradeoff ✅
- Agency circuits remain ACTIVE ✅
- Temporal binding intact ✅
- Can be overridden with sufficient motivation ✅

**Examples:**
- "It's raining" → deliberate choice not to go out
- "The gym is far" → weighed distance against benefit
- "My room is messy" → assessed cleanup effort

**Phenomenology:**
- "I decided not to" (active choice)
- "Not worth the effort" (cost-benefit)
- "Too much work right now" (threshold assessment)

**Why NOT C0:**
- Information successfully enters volitional system
- Deliberative processing occurs
- Agency is preserved throughout
- Cost-benefit weighing is explicit
- Can be overridden (e.g., emergency motivation)

**Neural Evidence:**
- Uses deliberative prefrontal-striatal circuits (Cell Reports 2025)
- ACC effort encoding (Westbrook & Braver 2015)
- Basal ganglia multiplex reward-effort-choice (2025 study)
- Metacognitive awareness present (confidence signals)
- Temporal binding normal (Caspar et al. 2016 comparison)

**Intervention:**
- Environmental redesign (reduce friction)
- Cost reframing (make effort seem less)
- Benefit amplification (increase perceived reward)
- Threshold adjustment (increase willingness)

---

### C3-9: Social Obligation (NEW - Boundary Category)

**Definition:** Mild to moderate social pressure, organizational hierarchy respect, normative conformity where agency is partially preserved.

**Mechanism:**
- Information enters prefrontal cortex ✅
- Identity-based gating: "people like me do/don't do this"
- Agency PARTIALLY preserved (can override with effort)
- Social conformity operates through identity constraints
- Ventral striatum treats conformity as rewarding

**Examples:**
- Boss makes reasonable request (non-coercive)
- Social expectation to attend wedding
- Professional norms and etiquette
- Peer pressure (mild to moderate)

**Phenomenology:**
- "I should" (obligation felt but choice present)
- "People expect it" (social norm awareness)
- "I can't not go" (identity constraint, not impossibility)

**Spectrum:**
- **Low intensity:** Easy to decline → clear C3-9
- **Moderate intensity:** Difficult but possible to decline → C3-9
- **High intensity:** Extreme pressure, threats → approaches C0c boundary

**Why NOT C0:**
- Volitional system engaged (not bypassed)
- Can choose differently (though costly)
- Reasons-responsiveness preserved
- Second-order volitions accessible

**Why NOT just C3:**
- Distinct mechanism (identity gating vs. effort costs)
- Social conformity research shows specific neural signature
- Requires separate diagnostic questions and interventions

**Intervention:**
- Autonomy support
- Identity reframing
- Social skills training for boundary-setting
- Values clarification (what do *you* want?)

## Part 4: Quick Classification Decision Tree

```
START: Person fails to act on stated intention
  ↓
Q1: Can the person perceive/represent the goal and action path?
  NO → Check for sensory/cognitive limitation → C0a
  YES → Continue
  ↓
Q2: Does constraint operate via subcortical override (<150ms)?
  YES → Check for trauma/threat response → C0b
  NO → Continue
  ↓
Q3: Is there a physical/biomechanical limitation that prevents execution?
  YES → Can any amount of intent overcome it?
    NO → C0a (Sensory/Physical Limitation)
    YES → Continue
  NO → Continue
  ↓
Q4: Does the person explicitly weigh costs/benefits before deciding?
  YES → Is constraint an effort/discomfort cost?
    YES → C3-8 (Environmental Friction)
    NO → Check other C3 categories
  NO → Continue
  ↓
Q5: Does the person feel they're CHOOSING or feel they MUST?
  CHOOSING → C3 (check which subcategory)
  MUST → Continue to Q6
  ↓
Q6: Is there explicit fear/threat if they don't comply?
  NO → C3-9 (Social Obligation)
  YES → How severe?
    MILD → C3-9
    SEVERE → C0c (Coercive Authority)
```

## Part 5: Diagnostic Questions by Category

### C0a: Healthy Pre-Volitional
- "If you had infinite energy and motivation, could you physically do this?"
- "Is there a physical/biological barrier preventing the action?"
- "Can your body execute the motor commands needed?"

### C0b: Trauma-Freeze
- "Do you experience sudden immobility when triggered?"
- "Does your body freeze before you can think about it?"
- "Do you feel disconnected from your body during these moments?"

### C0c: Coercive Authority
- "Are you afraid of consequences if you don't comply?"
- "Is there an explicit threat (job loss, harm, severe penalty)?"
- "Do you feel survival-level pressure to obey?"

### C3-8: Environmental Friction
- "Did you consciously weigh the effort against the benefit?"
- "Could you do it if the situation were different (no rain, closer gym)?"
- "Does it feel like a choice, even if an effortful one?"

### C3-9: Social Obligation
- "Could you choose differently if you were willing to face judgment?"
- "Is this about what others expect vs. what you want?"
- "Would you still do this if no one knew?"

## Part 7: Edge Cases and Resolutions

### Boss Commands (Context-Dependent)

**Scenario:** "My boss told me to attend a meeting."

**Analysis:**
- Information enters system? YES
- Coercion/threat present? DEPENDS
- Agency preserved? DEPENDS

**Classification:**
- **Reasonable request, no threat** → C3-9 (Social Obligation)
- **With implicit threat ("or else")** → Boundary between C3-9 and C0c
- **With explicit threat ("or you're fired")** → C0c (Coercive Authority)

**Test:** "If there were no consequences for declining, would you still feel compelled?"

### Strong Social Pressure

**Scenario:** "Everyone expects me to attend the wedding."

**Analysis:**
- Information enters? YES
- Fear/threat? MILD (judgment, not survival)
- Agency preserved? PARTIALLY (can decline but costly)

**Classification:** C3-9 (Social Obligation) with moderate intensity

**Test:** Could choose not to go (yes) → Not C0. Feels compelled by identity/norms (yes) → C3-9, not just C3-8.

### Complicated Processes

**Scenario:** "The application process is so complicated I can't complete it."

**Resolution depends on mechanism:**

**If complexity = cognitive effort:**
- Can be overcome with time/attention → C3-8 (Environmental Friction)
- Phenomenology: "This is hard but I can figure it out"

**If complexity = intentionally designed deterrent:**
- Authority-designed barrier → C3-9 or C0c depending on severity
- Phenomenology: "They made this impossible"

**Test:** "Who designed this complexity and why?"

### Internalized Norms

**Scenario:** "I can't wear white after Labor Day" (internalized fashion rule).

**Analysis:**
- Original source: Social (external)
- Current mechanism: Internalized identity constraint
- Agency: Can be consciously examined and rejected

**Classification:** C3-9 (Social Obligation) - even when internalized, operates through identity gating

**Test:** "If all external pressure disappeared, would you still follow this rule?"
- **Yes (genuinely integrated)** → May have migrated to autonomous motivation (borderline C0/C3)
- **No (complying to avoid judgment)** → Clear C3-9

### Sustained Trauma Freeze

**Scenario:** "I freeze when triggered, lasting 30+ seconds."

**Analysis - Two Phases:**

**Phase 1: Initial Trigger (0-150ms)**
- Subcortical amygdala→PAG pathway
- Bypasses cortical evaluation
- Complete agency suppression
- **Classification:** C0b (Trauma-Freeze)

**Phase 2: Sustained Freeze (150ms+)**
- Cortical circuits engage
- vmPFC can modulate duration
- Perceived controllability affects maintenance
- **Classification:** C0b origin, but maintenance involves volitional modulation

**Resolution:** Classify as C0b because initial trigger is pre-volitional, even though sustained freeze involves some volitional processing.

## Part 8: Evidence Base Summary

### Neuroscience Support

**Environmental Friction = C3:**
- Cell Reports 2025: Multi-level encoding of reward, effort, and choice across frontal cortex and basal ganglia
- Westbrook & Braver 2015: ACC encodes effort costs through deliberative valuation
- Caspar et al. 2016, 2024: Temporal binding intact during effort-based decisions
- Metacognitive awareness preserved (confidence encoding in mPFC)

**Authority Compliance = C0c (when severe):**
- Caspar et al. 2016, 2024: Authority compliance reduces temporal binding, alters mPFC activity
- Milgram paradigm: "Agentic state" where responsibility is diffused
- Reduced N1 amplitude = diminished outcome processing
- Lowered mid-frontal theta = less cognitive conflict

**Social Conformity = C3-9:**
- pMFC detects conflict with group opinion
- Ventral striatum treats conformity as rewarding
- Social prediction error signals drive automatic alignment
- Gates alternatives but maintains volitional engagement

### Behavioral Economics Support

**Thaler & Sunstein Framework:**
- **Nudges** (environmental design) ≠ **Mandates** (forbid options)
- **Sludge** (friction) ≠ **Social norms** (obligation)
- Explicit distinction validates C3-8 vs. C0c boundary

**Transaction Costs vs. Social Costs:**
- Transaction costs (search, effort) = C3-8
- Social costs (reputation, relationships) = C3-9
- Coase/Williamson: Different mechanisms require different institutional responses

### Philosophical Support

**Five Frameworks Converge:**
1. **Frankfurt's Hierarchy:** Second-order volitions intact (C3) vs. overridden (C0)
2. **Self-Determination Theory:** Autonomous (C3) vs. controlled motivation (C0c when severe)
3. **Fischer & Ravizza:** Reasons-responsiveness preserved (C3) vs. compromised (C0)
4. **Berlin's Liberty:** Positive liberty (C3 capabilities) vs. negative liberty (C0 constraints)
5. **Coercion Theory:** Will-overbearing (C0c) vs. making less attractive (C3)

**5-Criterion Algorithm for Edge Cases:**
1. **Source:** Agent-imposed vs. natural/circumstantial
2. **Mechanism:** First-order execution vs. second-order volition
3. **Reasons-responsiveness:** Preserved vs. compromised
4. **Autonomy experience:** Willingly endorsing vs. feeling compelled
5. **Will-overbearing:** Makes less attractive vs. overrides will

**Decision rule:** 3+ criteria agreement = classification
