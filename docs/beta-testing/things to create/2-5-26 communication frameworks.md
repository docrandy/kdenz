The Greatest Insights & Techniques
1. For structuring thoughts on the spot
• The Pyramid Principle: When speaking to executives or answering high-stakes questions, start with your main recommendation or answer first. Follow this with your reasons, and provide details last,.
• PREP Framework: For sharing opinions, state your Point, give a Reason, provide an Example, and restate your Point.
• The 3-2-1 Method: To master small talk, keep answers brief by thinking in frameworks: "There are 3 steps," "There are 2 types," or "The 1 thing about this is...".
2. For commanding attention and authority
• The Power Pause: Silence shows control. Pause before answering a question, after a key point, or after a bold statement to make people lean in. Research suggests waiting up to eight seconds after asking for a sale increases closing rates by 30%.
• Physical & Vocal Presence: To stop people from interrupting you, increase your volume and use larger hand gestures. If you are in a meeting, stand up to deliver your point; it makes it physically harder for others to interrupt,.
• The "One Thing" Technique: Instead of listing vague ideas, zoom in and use authoritative language like "The single most important thing is..." to signal clarity,.
3. For perfect delivery and tone
• The 3 Constants of Sales Tone: To maximize comprehension and trust, you must speak loud enough to be heard, slow enough to be understood (ideally 135–185 words per minute), and articulate every word clearly,.
• Matching and Mirroring: Build rapport by matching the other person's energy and volume initially ("meeting them where they are"), then slowly leading them to the energy level you want,.
• Variable Pitch: Use your voice's frequency to dictate meaning. An upward inflection signals a question (soliciting a response), while a downward inflection signals a command or statement,.
4. For training and preparation
• "Breathing the Script": To sound natural while using a script, read it aloud and cross out one word at a time, repeating the process until the page is blacked out. This helps you memorize the flow so you can focus on the prospect rather than the words,.
• The Recovery Rep: Practice overcoming the fear of freezing by pretending to blank out during a story on purpose. Say "Wait, where was I?" to realize that the audience will usually help you rather than judge you.
