# C0/C3 Boundary Refinement Based on Behavioral Economics Research

**Date:** 2026-02-10
**Status:** Research-Based Recommendations
**Related:** `behavioral_economics_constraint_taxonomy.md`

---

## Executive Summary

Behavioral economics research **strongly supports** the VCM distinction between C0 (Environmental Friction) and C3 (Social/Authority Constraints) as **fundamentally different** gate types. The field explicitly categorizes these as operating through distinct mechanisms and requiring different analytical frameworks.

**Key Finding:** Authority mandates and social norms are **NOT** high-friction environmental constraints. They are qualitatively different constraint types.

---

## Current VCM Definitions

### C0: Environmental Friction
"The action is physically or logistically difficult, time-consuming, or requires resources I don't readily have."

**Mechanism:** Effort costs, distance, time, cognitive load
**Navigability:** Can be overcome with sufficient effort/motivation/resources
**Example:** "The gym is far away"

### C3: Social and Authority Constraints
"Doing this would defy expectations, norms, or direct commands from others (authority figures, social groups, or cultural contexts)."

**Mechanism:** Norms, legitimacy, identity, obligation, compliance pressure
**Navigability:** Can be violated, but incurs social/reputational/authority penalties
**Example:** "My boss explicitly told me not to do this"

---

## Behavioral Economics Evidence for Distinction

### 1. Thaler & Sunstein: Mandates ≠ Sludge

**Mandates (C3):**
- **Forbid options** entirely
- Operate through **command/compliance**
- Not reducible to effort costs

**Sludge (C0):**
- Increases **effort/friction**
- Options remain available (just harder)
- Can be overcome with persistence

**Quote:** "To count as a mere nudge, the intervention must be easy and cheap to avoid. Nudges are not mandates. Putting fruit at eye level counts as a nudge. Banning junk food does not."

**Implication:** Banning ≠ making difficult. Authority constraints (C3) are categorically different from environmental friction (C0).

---

### 2. Friction Taxonomy: Multiple Distinct Types

**Physical/Cognitive Friction (C0):**
- Distance, time, effort, cognitive load
- Overcome with resources/motivation

**Social Friction (C3):**
- "Resistance within social systems opposing change"
- "Arises from established social structures, cultural norms"
- **Explicitly distinct** mechanism from physical friction

**Quote:** "Social friction is the measurable resistance within a social system that opposes a change in state... arising from the interplay between established social structures, cultural norms, economic incentives."

**Implication:** Social friction is NOT reducible to physical/cognitive friction. Different mechanism, different analytical framework.

---

### 3. Transaction Costs vs Social Costs

**Transaction Costs (C0):**
- Search, bargaining, monitoring costs
- Arise from bounded rationality and imperfect information
- Effort-based

**Social Costs (C3):**
- Externalities imposed on others
- Reputational, relational, communal impacts
- **Not the same** as transaction costs

**Quote:** "Corporate hierarchies may successfully exercise their self-regulatory role by economizing on transaction costs, yet in doing so they generate social costs that are reducible through the self-regulatory activity of public institutions."

**Implication:** Transaction costs and social costs are **distinct economic categories**, not points on a friction spectrum.

---

### 4. Kelman's Social Influence Theory: Vertical vs Horizontal

**Vertical Influence (C3):**
- "Conformity to presumed demands by **authority**"
- Operates through **legitimacy and obligation**
- Not reducible to effort

**Horizontal Influence (C3):**
- "Learning that observed agents follow the norm very closely"
- Operates through **social learning and comparison**
- Distinct from environmental navigation

**Key Insight:** Both vertical and horizontal social influence are **distinct mechanisms** from environmental friction.

---

### 5. Identity and Social Norms (Akerlof & Kranton)

**Finding:** "People make choices to conform to social norms consistent with their identities. Conformity is especially pronounced in **public spheres**."

**Mechanism:**
- **Anonymizing choices** reduces conformity
- Indicates **preference mechanisms** (social) vs. **belief mechanisms** (informational)
- Not about effort to coordinate, but about **identity and reputation**

**Implication:** Social conformity operates through identity/reputation mechanisms, NOT effort minimization.

---

### 6. Authority Compliance: Legitimacy, Not Just Cost

**Two Types of Authority:**
1. **Authority "to"** - Legitimate power (command compliance)
2. **Authority "in"** - Expert knowledge (informational influence)

**Quote:** "Authority and the behavioral response to authority is central to many important questions in public economics."

**Mechanism:** Compliance to authority cues is a "powerful motivating mechanism" operating through **perceived legitimacy and obligation**.

**Implication:** Authority compliance involves **obligation**, not just increased effort to resist. Categorically different from environmental navigation.

---

### 7. Self-Control vs External Control

**Self-Control (Internal):**
- "Farsighted planner" vs "myopic doer"
- Personal rules, internal incentives
- **Self-imposed** constraints

**External Control (C3):**
- Other-imposed expectations/commands
- Social/authority pressure
- **Other-imposed** constraints

**Key Distinction:** C0 environmental friction can be navigated with **self-control and effort**. C3 social/authority constraints come from **external agents** and require accepting external consequences (not just expending effort).

## Refined Diagnostic Criteria

### C0: Environmental Friction

**Primary Question:** "How hard is it to execute the action **physically/cognitively**?"

**Characteristics:**
- Measurable in effort, time, distance, cognitive load, resources
- Overcome with motivation/resources/persistence
- **Cost type:** Opportunity cost (time, energy, attention)
- **Barrier location:** In the physical/logistical environment
- **Violation consequence:** Task remains incomplete or requires more effort

**Examples:**
- "The gym is 30 minutes away"
- "This requires tools I don't have"
- "The form has 50 questions"
- "This would take 3 hours"
- "I'd need to learn new software"

**Test:** If I had infinite energy and time, could I do this without social consequences? **YES → C0**

---

### C3: Social and Authority Constraints

**Primary Question:** "What **social or institutional penalties** apply if I do this?"

**Characteristics:**
- Measurable in reputational costs, relationship damage, sanctions, legitimacy loss
- Overcome by accepting social/authority consequences
- **Cost type:** Reputational cost, relational cost, legitimacy cost, compliance cost
- **Barrier location:** In social/institutional systems
- **Violation consequence:** Social penalty (judgment, exclusion, sanction) OR authority penalty (discipline, punishment)

**Examples:**
- "My boss explicitly told me not to"
- "This violates company policy"
- "Everyone would think I'm selfish"
- "This goes against our cultural norms"
- "I'd be kicked out of the group"

**Test:** If I had infinite energy and time, could I do this without social consequences? **NO → C3**

## Boundary Cases and Resolution

### Case 1: "The process is really complicated"

**If complexity = cognitive effort barrier:**
- Mechanism: Bounded rationality, cognitive load
- Overcomable: With time, attention, assistance
- **Classification: C0** (environmental friction / sludge)

**If complexity = intentional institutional barrier:**
- Mechanism: Authority-imposed deterrent
- Purpose: Discourage action through frustration
- **Classification: C3** (institutional constraint / sludge with authority)

**Resolution Rule:** Ask "Who designed this complexity?"
- Emergent complexity from task nature → C0
- Designed complexity from authority to discourage → C3

**Example:**
- "Building a rocket is complicated" → C0 (inherent task complexity)
- "The boss made the approval form intentionally complex to discourage requests" → C3 (authority deterrent)

---

### Case 2: "Everyone else is doing X, so I'd stand out"

**If standing out = effort to explain/coordinate:**
- Mechanism: Informational friction, coordination costs
- Cost: Time explaining, cognitive load of being "the different one"
- **Classification: C0** (coordination friction)

**If standing out = norm violation penalty:**
- Mechanism: Social disapproval, judgment, exclusion risk
- Cost: Reputational damage, relationship strain
- **Classification: C3** (social constraint)

**Resolution Rule:** Ask "What's the cost of standing out?"
- Effort to explain/coordinate → C0
- Social judgment/exclusion → C3

**Example:**
- "Everyone uses Slack for communication, so I'd have to explain why I'm not checking it" → C0 (coordination cost)
- "Everyone dresses formally, so I'd be judged as unprofessional if I wore casual clothes" → C3 (social norm violation)

---

### Case 3: "This requires asking permission from my manager"

**If manager approval = logistical step:**
- Mechanism: Process requirement, waiting time
- Cost: Time waiting, effort to submit request
- **Classification: C0** (process friction)

**If manager approval = authority gate:**
- Mechanism: Power hierarchy, legitimate authority
- Cost: Risk of denial, need to justify, deference required
- **Classification: C3** (authority constraint)

**Resolution Rule:** Ask "What happens if I skip asking?"
- Task doesn't get done (logistical blocker) → C0
- I violated authority chain (insubordination) → C3

**Example:**
- "I need manager approval to access the file server" → C0 (logistical: manager has password)
- "I need manager approval to change the policy" → C3 (authority: manager has legitimate power)

---

### Case 4: "It's company policy"

**If policy = procedural standard:**
- Mechanism: Coordination, standardization
- Purpose: Efficiency, consistency
- Violation: Creates confusion, inefficiency
- **Classification: C0** (process friction)

**If policy = authoritative rule:**
- Mechanism: Command from authority
- Purpose: Control, compliance
- Violation: Insubordination, sanctions
- **Classification: C3** (authority constraint / institutional rule)

**Resolution Rule:** Ask "What's enforced?"
- Best practices for efficiency → C0 (process)
- Obedience to authority → C3 (institutional)

**Example:**
- "Company policy says to use the template" → C0 (standardization for efficiency; violation = extra work)
- "Company policy prohibits this, and you'll be disciplined" → C3 (authority rule; violation = sanction)

## Decision Tree for C0 vs C3

```
START: "I'm experiencing a barrier to action"
  |
  V
Question 1: Is this barrier imposed by another agent (person, authority, social group)?
  |
  ├─ NO → C0 (environmental friction)
  |
  └─ YES
      |
      V
Question 2: If I had infinite energy/time/resources, could I do this without social/authority consequences?
      |
      ├─ YES → C0 (it's actually logistical friction, not social/authority)
      |
      └─ NO
          |
          V
Question 3: What type of cost would I incur?
          |
          ├─ Physical/cognitive effort, time, coordination complexity → C0
          |
          └─ Social disapproval, reputational damage, authority sanction → C3
```

## Refinement Recommendations

### Update C0 Definition

**Current:**
"The action is physically or logistically difficult, time-consuming, or requires resources I don't readily have."

**Recommended Addition:**
"The action is physically or logistically difficult, time-consuming, or requires resources I don't readily have. **The barrier is in the environment (physical, temporal, or cognitive), not in social or authority systems. Overcoming it requires effort/resources, not accepting social/authority consequences.**"

**Clarification:** C0 includes:
- Physical distance, effort, energy requirements
- Time requirements, scheduling complexity
- Resource availability (tools, money, information)
- Cognitive load, skill requirements, learning curves
- Process complexity, coordination costs
- Informational friction (not knowing how)

**Exclusion:** C0 does NOT include:
- Social disapproval or reputational costs (→ C3)
- Authority prohibitions or sanctions (→ C3)
- Norm violation penalties (→ C3)
- Legitimacy or obligation concerns (→ C3)

---

### Update C3 Definition

**Current:**
"Doing this would defy expectations, norms, or direct commands from others (authority figures, social groups, or cultural contexts)."

**Recommended Addition:**
"Doing this would defy expectations, norms, or direct commands from others (authority figures, social groups, or cultural contexts). **The barrier comes from external agents imposing social or institutional expectations. Overcoming it requires accepting social/reputational costs or authority sanctions, not just expending effort.**"

**Clarification:** C3 includes:
- **Authority constraints:** Commands, rules, prohibitions, policies (from legitimate authority)
- **Social norms:** Expectations, conventions, standards (from social groups)
- **Reputational costs:** Judgment, disapproval, exclusion risks
- **Identity threats:** Actions inconsistent with social identity
- **Compliance pressure:** Vertical (authority) or horizontal (peer) influence

**Exclusion:** C3 does NOT include:
- Coordination complexity that's purely logistical (→ C0)
- Process requirements without authority enforcement (→ C0)
- Informational influence (learning from others about facts) (→ C2)

---

### Add Diagnostic Guidance to Documentation

**Boundary Case Decision Heuristics:**

1. **"Who/what is imposing the barrier?"**
   - Physical world, task nature, logistics → C0
   - Social group, authority figure, institutional system → C3

2. **"What type of cost increases if I proceed anyway?"**
   - Effort, time, resources → C0
   - Reputation, relationships, compliance standing → C3

3. **"Could infinite effort/resources overcome this without social consequences?"**
   - Yes → C0
   - No → C3

4. **"Is this barrier about capability or permission/approval?"**
   - Capability (can I do this?) → C0
   - Permission (should I do this given social/authority expectations?) → C3

## Key Insights from Behavioral Economics

### What This Research Confirms

1. **Distinct Mechanisms:** C0 and C3 operate through fundamentally different psychological and social mechanisms.

2. **Not a Spectrum:** Authority/social constraints are not "high-friction environmental constraints." They're categorically different.

3. **Policy Implications:** Different constraint types require different interventions:
   - C0: Reduce sludge, add resources, simplify processes
   - C3: Change norms, reframe authority, alter social incentives

4. **Measurement Differences:**
   - C0: Measurable in time, distance, effort, cognitive load
   - C3: Measurable in reputational risk, relational cost, sanction severity

5. **Well-Established Frameworks:** Multiple independent frameworks (Thaler/Sunstein, Kelman, Coase, Newell) all distinguish these categories.

### What This Research Does NOT Support

1. **Conflation:** No evidence for treating mandates/norms as merely "very high friction."

2. **Unified Friction Model:** Behavioral economics does NOT use a single "friction" construct for all constraints.

3. **Social Costs as Transaction Costs:** Social costs and transaction costs are explicitly distinguished in institutional economics.

## Implementation Recommendations

### For VCM Gate Detection

**C0 Detection Keywords:**
- "far away," "takes too long," "need tools," "don't have time," "too complicated," "requires learning"
- "process is complex," "logistics are difficult," "coordination is hard"

**C3 Detection Keywords:**
- "boss said no," "against policy," "violates norms," "people would judge," "not allowed"
- "everyone does X," "expected to," "authority requires," "would be unprofessional"

**Boundary Case Flags:**
- "complicated process" → Check: Inherent complexity (C0) or authority-imposed deterrent (C3)?
- "stand out" → Check: Coordination effort (C0) or social judgment (C3)?
- "policy requires" → Check: Process standard (C0) or authority rule (C3)?

### For User Feedback

**When presenting C0:**
- "This is about the **effort, time, or resources** required."
- "The barrier is **logistical or practical**."

**When presenting C3:**
- "This is about **social or authority expectations**."
- "The barrier is **relational or institutional**."

**When clarifying boundary:**
- "Is the challenge the **work required** (C0) or the **social/authority consequences** (C3)?"

## Conclusion

Behavioral economics research provides **strong validation** for the VCM framework's distinction between C0 (Environmental Friction) and C3 (Social/Authority Constraints). The field consistently treats these as:

- **Mechanistically distinct:** Different psychological/social processes
- **Analytically separate:** Different frameworks, different measurement
- **Policy-relevant:** Different interventions required

**Recommendation:** Maintain C0/C3 as distinct gate categories. Add clarifying language to emphasize the **mechanism distinction** (effort/resources vs. social/authority consequences) and provide **diagnostic heuristics** for boundary cases.

The research supports **refining** the boundary through clearer operational definitions, NOT **collapsing** the categories.
